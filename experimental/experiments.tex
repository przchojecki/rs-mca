\begin{abstract}
This replacement starts with the current ``fresh eyes'' synthesis for the
smooth Reed--Solomon Proximity Prize problem.  The negative side is now quite
rigid: Paper D v5 gives a prize-facing upper cap on the MCA threshold through a
self-contained deep-point route, while Paper A and the list lower bounds rule
out any no-slack capacity theorem.  The positive side is not yet a theorem; it
should be organized around two local-limit problems, L1 arbitrary-word locator
fibers and M1 all-line residue packing, plus extension-line MCA, Paper D
conversion/radius audits, and exact protocol-ledger rewrites.

The main new additions over the old \texttt{experiments.tex} are two compact
bridge lemmas.  First, for ordinary column-distance interleaving, interleaved
lists inject into common feasible supports, so a uniform base locator-fiber
theorem gives the same polynomial exponent for all protocol arities; there is no
automatic \(n^{\mu B}\) product loss.  Second, a list bound plus an MCA bound
implies a concrete line-decoding bound with loss \(M+nL+1\).  The older BCHKS
provenance notes, quotient-locator dictionaries, normal forms, arithmetic gates,
and finite-threshold infrastructure are retained below whenever they still look
useful.
\end{abstract}

\section{Fresh-Eyes Settlement Memo}
\label{sec:fresh-eyes-settlement-memo}

I would frame the current state as follows.  The attached suite substantially
settles the negative side and gives a plausible corrected theorem shape, but it
does not yet settle the Proximity Prize threshold in the sharp ``largest
\(\delta^*\)'' sense.  The clean prize-relevant statement is no longer
``prove smooth RS reaches capacity''.  It is
\[
  \delta=1-\rho-\eta,
  \qquad
  \eta\text{ must clear generated-field entropy, quotient-core, MCA-floor,}
\]
\[
  \text{interleaving, line-field, and challenge-field budgets.}
\]
For
\[
  C=\RS[\F,D,k],\qquad n=|D|,
  \qquad \rho=k/n\in\{1/2,1/4,1/8,1/16\},
\]
the official challenge statements use this MCA/list-threshold form; in this
experimental ledger the concrete prize envelope is~\cite{ProximityPrize26}
\[
  \epsstar=2^{-128},\qquad k\le2^{40},\qquad |\F|<2^{256}.
\]
The protocol-relevant soundness contribution has the schematic form
\[
  \epsmca(C,\delta)
  +\frac{|\Lam(C^{\equiv\mu},\delta)|}{\qline}
  +\text{query error}.
\]
Thus a list theorem alone is not enough unless it is connected to the exact MCA,
correlated-agreement, line-decoding, or curve-MCA quantity used by the protocol,
and unless the denominator is the actual line or challenge field.

\subsection{Bottom line}

For the \textbf{MCA/correlated-agreement threshold}, the strongest attached
result is Paper D v5's universal cap.  Through the self-contained deep-point
route and the stated dyadic-divisor hypotheses,
\[
  \delta^*_{\rm MCA}(2^{-128})
  \le 1-\rho-2^{-9}
  \quad (\rho=1/2,1/4,1/8),
\]
and
\[
  \delta^*_{\rm MCA}(2^{-128})
  \le 1-\rho-2^{-10}
  \quad (\rho=1/16).
\]
Equivalently, throughout the relevant unsafe intervals Paper D gives
\[
  \epsmca(C,\delta)
  >\frac1{2k}\left(1-\frac n{|\F|}\right)
  \ge2^{-86}\gg2^{-128}.
\]
This is a real prize-facing cap, not merely a heuristic obstruction.  It is not
an error-one theorem; it proves failure probability on the order of \(1/k\),
which is already far above \(2^{-128}\), but it does not characterize the full
failure curve.

For the \textbf{interleaved-list threshold}, Paper A's base list lower bound
plus the base-to-interleaved monotonicity gives a clean negative cap.  At quotient
scale \(N\mid n\), with \(\rho N\in\mathbb Z\), there is a received word with at
least
\[
  \binom{N}{\rho N+1}/q,
  \qquad q=|\F|,
\]
base-code codewords at radius \(1-\rho-1/N\).  Therefore the same radius is
unsafe for the interleaved-list budget whenever this exceeds \(2^{-128}q\).  In
the worst case \(q<2^{256}\), the following choices suffice:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\hline
\rho & N & \text{list-failure radius} &
\log_2\binom{N}{\rho N+1}-256 &
\text{margin over }2^{128}\\
\hline
1/2  & 512  & 1/2-2^{-9}    & 251.17 & 123.17\\
1/4  & 512  & 3/4-2^{-9}    & 156.33 & 28.33\\
1/8  & 1024 & 7/8-2^{-10}   & 298.68 & 170.68\\
1/16 & 2048 & 15/16-2^{-11} & 433.89 & 305.89\\
\hline
\end{array}
\]
The fourth column is the exponent of the lower-bound list size after dividing by
the worst-case field size \(2^{256}\); the fifth subtracts the target exponent
\(128\).

\subsection{What is already proved or very close to proved}

The old no-slack smooth-domain capacity claim is dead.  The quotient-locator
construction gives explicit bad slopes for canonical lines and, over deployed
smooth prime fields such as BabyBear, KoalaBear, and \(3\cdot2^{30}+1\), yields
support-wise line-MCA error one on intervals starting at
\(1-\rho-2^{-18}\) for all four prize rates, with the sharper \(2^{-17}\) gap
at rates \(1/2\) and \(1/4\).  These statements rule out any theorem saying
that every radius below \(1-\rho\) has negligible MCA error.

Paper D is the most prize-relevant negative theorem because it converts a deep
point/list phenomenon into MCA at the exact \(2^{-128}\)-style threshold.  In
v5, the headline MCA cap is routed locally through a self-contained deep-point
argument rather than the Crites--Stewart list-to-agreement import.  The remaining
CA/list comparison and fallback statements still need their own radius,
normalization, augmented-code, and import audits.

The list lower-bound side is also stronger than it is currently advertised.  It
should be promoted to an explicit interleaved-list negative corollary: base lists
embed into ordinary interleaved lists, and the binomial numerators above already
exceed the \(2^{-128}|\F|\) budget in the full field envelope for the displayed
quotient divisors.

\subsection{The corrected positive theorem shape}

The positive theorem should use
\[
  a=\lceil(1-\delta)n\rceil=k+\sigma,
  \qquad \delta=1-\rho-\eta,
\]
and require at least
\[
  \sigma\log_2\qgen
  \ge (1+\varepsilon)\log_2\binom n{k+\sigma},
  \qquad
  \sigma\ge C\frac n{\log n},
\]
plus explicit quotient-profile and failure-floor ledgers.  The first inequality
uses the generated field \(\qgen\), not a larger extension or challenge field
that merely appears later in the protocol.  The MCA numerator must be divided by
the actual line field \(\qline\).

The intended list target is an arbitrary-word locator-fiber theorem
\[
  \sup_U |\Fib_U(k+\sigma)|\le n^B.
\]
The intended MCA target is an all-line bound of the form
\[
  \epsmca(C,1-\rho-\eta)
  \le
  \frac{
    n^{1+o(1)}
    +2^{(\beta(\rho)/\Htwo(\rho))\QH(\eta)(1+o(1))}
  }{\qline},
\]
where quotient-periodic classes are removed or charged by \(\QH(\eta)\).  The
current packet contains important special cases and reductions, but not these
full arbitrary-word and all-line theorems.

\subsection{What remains missing}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{L1: arbitrary-word locator local limit.}  This is the real list
upper-bound theorem.  Characteristic-zero rigidity, monomial-prefix lanes, and
split-prime transfer results are useful but do not yet imply the uniform
polynomial-field theorem needed for arbitrary received words.

\item \textbf{M1: all-line aperiodic residue packing.}  Canonical-line exactness
and quotient floors are lower-bound tools, not all-line upper bounds.  The
positive MCA theorem needs a fixed-field, all-line, all-base residue-packing
bound.

\item \textbf{F1: extension-line MCA.}  The list side has a clean extension-code
identity.  MCA does not.  Base-valued bad lines can deflate when sampled over an
extension field, while Paper D's cap indicates that genuinely extension-valued
bad lines exist in relevant regimes.  Any extension-line lift must therefore be
a corrected-reserve theorem, not a blanket subfield statement.

\item \textbf{Paper D conversion audit.}  Paper D v5's headline MCA cap is no
longer load-bearing on the direct Crites--Stewart conversion.  The self-contained
deep-point step, support-wise monotonicity, integer-radius conventions, and the
remaining CA/list import statements should still be independently checked.

\item \textbf{Protocol-ledger rewrite.}  Deployed FRI/WHIR/IOP analyses must be
rewritten with exact terms
\[
  |\Lam(C^{\equiv\mu},\delta)|/\qline
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \epsmca\text{ or line-decoding over the actual line field}.
\]
The survey toy protocol already has this shape; deployed analyses still need the
same ledger.
\end{enumerate}

\section{New Bridge I: Common-Support Interleaved Fiber Injection}
\label{sec:common-support-interleaved-fiber-injection}

\textbf{Status: \textsc{New-local} wrapper.}
The older ledger treated interleaving conservatively through a safe product
bound \(L_\mu(\delta)\le L_1(\delta)^\mu\).  For ordinary column-distance RS
interleaving, the following support-level injection gives the sharper upper
bound: once the one-row locator-fiber theorem is proved, interleaving costs no
extra exponent.

For \(S\subseteq D\), let
\[
  L_S(X)=\prod_{x\in S}(X-x).
\]
For a received word \(U\in\F^D\), the condition \(\deg(U\bmod L_S)<k\) means
that the unique polynomial of degree less than \(|S|\) interpolating \(U|_S\)
has degree less than \(k\).

\begin{definition}[one-row and common interleaved fibers]
Let \(C=\RS[\F,D,k]\), let \(a\ge k\), and let
\(\mathbf U=(U_1,\ldots,U_\mu)\in(\F^\mu)^D\).  Define
\[
  \Fib^{(\mu)}_{\mathbf U}(a)
  =
  \{S\subseteq D:
    |S|=a,\ \deg(U_i\bmod L_S)<k
    \text{ for every }i=1,\ldots,\mu\}.
\]
For one row, write \(\Fib_{U_i}(a)\) for the same condition with only \(U_i\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[common-support interleaved fiber injection]
\label{lem:common-support-interleaved-fiber-injection}
Let \(C=\RS[\F,D,k]\), \(n=|D|\), and let
\(a=\lceil(1-\delta)n\rceil\ge k\).  For every received \(\mu\)-row word
\(\mathbf U\in(\F^\mu)^D\), ordinary column-distance interleaving satisfies
\[
  |\Lam(C^{\equiv\mu},\delta,\mathbf U)|
  \le |\Fib^{(\mu)}_{\mathbf U}(a)|
  \le \min_{1\le i\le\mu}|\Fib_{U_i}(a)|.
\]
Consequently,
\[
  \sup_{\mathbf U}|\Lam(C^{\equiv\mu},\delta,\mathbf U)|
  \le \sup_U |\Fib_U(a)|.
\]
In particular, if L1 proves \(\sup_U|\Fib_U(k+\sigma)|\le n^B\), then
\[
  |\Lam(C^{\equiv\mu},1-\rho-\sigma/n)|\le n^B
\]
for every ordinary column-distance protocol arity \(\mu\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a total order on \(D\).  A listed interleaved codeword
\((P_1,\ldots,P_\mu)\in C^{\equiv\mu}\) at column distance at most \(\delta\) from
\(\mathbf U\) agrees with \(\mathbf U\) on at least \(a\) common columns.  Choose
the first \(a\)-subset \(S\) of those agreeing columns.  For this \(S\), every
row \(U_i|_S\) is interpolated by the degree-\(<k\) polynomial \(P_i\), hence
\(S\in\Fib^{(\mu)}_{\mathbf U}(a)\).

For fixed \(S\), the tuple \((P_1,\ldots,P_\mu)\) is uniquely determined,
because each \(P_i\) has degree less than \(k\) and is fixed by its values on
\(|S|\ge k\) points.  Thus the canonical-support map from listed interleaved
codewords to common feasible supports is injective.  The second inequality is
immediate because every common feasible support is feasible for each individual
row.  Taking suprema gives the final claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[impact]
For ordinary column-distance RS interleaving, L2 should be demoted from a
separate product-exponent problem to a corollary of L1.  The hard list theorem
remains the base arbitrary-word locator local limit.
\end{remark}

\section{New Bridge II: List plus MCA Gives Line Decoding}
\label{sec:list-plus-mca-gives-line-decoding}

\textbf{Status: \textsc{New-local} bridge.}
The next proposition turns a line-decoding proof obligation into a finite-constant
budget once L1 and M1 are available.  It uses finite slopes and the support-wise
MCA normalization used in this packet.

\begin{proposition}[list/MCA bridge to line decoding]
\label{prop:list-mca-to-line-decoding-bridge}
Let \(C=\RS[\F,D,k]\), let \(|D|=n\), and let
\(a=\lceil(1-\delta)n\rceil>k\).  Suppose that every allowed received line
\(f+\gamma g\) has at most \(M\) support-wise MCA-bad finite slopes at radius
\(\delta\), equivalently \(\epsmca(C,\delta)\le M/|\F|\) for the chosen finite
slope sampler.  Suppose also that the two-row interleaved list size satisfies
\[
  |\Lam(C^{\equiv2},\delta,W)|\le L
  \qquad\text{for every }W\in(\F^2)^D.
\]
Then \(C\) is line-decodable with parameters
\[
  (\delta,\ M+nL+1,\ n+1)
\]
in the following sense: if an allowed received line \(f+\gamma g\) has more than
\(M+nL\) finite slopes \(\gamma\) that are \(\delta\)-close to selected codewords
\(U(\gamma)\in C\), then there are codewords \(c_1,c_2\in C\) such that
\[
  U(\gamma)=c_1+\gamma c_2
\]
for at least \(n+1\) of those slopes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each close slope \(\gamma\), choose a support \(S_\gamma\subseteq D\) of size
\(a\) on which \(f+\gamma g\) agrees with the selected codeword \(U(\gamma)\).
Discard the at most \(M\) support-wise MCA-bad slopes.  For every remaining
slope, support-wise containment gives codewords \(c_{1,\gamma},c_{2,\gamma}\in C\)
such that
\[
  c_{1,\gamma}|_{S_\gamma}=f|_{S_\gamma},
  \qquad
  c_{2,\gamma}|_{S_\gamma}=g|_{S_\gamma}.
\]
Thus \((c_{1,\gamma},c_{2,\gamma})\) is a two-row interleaved codeword
\(\delta\)-close to the received two-row word \((f,g)\), using the common support
\(S_\gamma\).  There are at most \(L\) possible pairs.

If the original line has more than \(M+nL\) close slopes, then after discarding
bad slopes more than \(nL\) slopes remain.  By pigeonhole, one pair
\((c_1,c_2)\) occurs for at least \(n+1\) slopes.  For each such slope, the
codewords \(U(\gamma)\) and \(c_1+\gamma c_2\) both agree with \(f+\gamma g\) on
\(S_\gamma\), hence agree with each other on \(a>k\) positions.  Since both have
degree less than \(k\), they are identical codewords.  This proves the stated
line-decoding conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[finite-constant consequence]
If M1 gives \(M=n^{1+o(1)}\) and L1 plus
Lemma~\ref{lem:common-support-interleaved-fiber-injection} gives \(L=n^B\), then
Proposition~\ref{prop:list-mca-to-line-decoding-bridge} gives
\[
  M+nL+1\le n^{B+1+o(1)}.
\]
For \(n\le2^{44}\) and \(|\F|<2^{256}\), a raw list numerator \(n^B\) is
compatible with a \(2^{-128}\) field budget only for about \(B\lesssim2.9\), and
the line-decoding bridge is compatible only for about \(B\lesssim1.9\).  Thus the
bridge is useful, but it leaves a real finite-constant target.
\end{remark}

\section{Updated Positive-Theorem Package}
\label{sec:updated-positive-theorem-package}

The clean theorem to aim for is the following finite-reserve package.  Let
\[
  C=\RS[\F_{\rm line},D,k],\qquad n=|D|,
  \qquad a=k+\sigma,
  \qquad \delta=1-a/n=1-\rho-\eta.
\]
Assume
\[
  \sigma\log_2\qgen
  \ge (1+\varepsilon)\log_2\binom n{k+\sigma},
  \qquad
  \sigma\ge C_{\rho,B,\varepsilon}\frac n{\log n},
\]
the quotient profile \(\QH(\eta)\) is empty or explicitly budgeted, \(\eta\) lies
above all proved failure floors including Paper D's universal-cap floor, and the
MCA or line experiment is proved over \(\qline\), not merely over \(\qchal\).

\begin{conjecture}[corrected finite reserve package]
\label{conj:corrected-finite-reserve-package}
Under the preceding hypotheses, the desired conclusions are
\[
  |\Lam(C^{\equiv\mu},\delta)|\le n^B
\]
for all ordinary column-distance protocol arities \(\mu\), by
Lemma~\ref{lem:common-support-interleaved-fiber-injection}; and
\[
  \epsmca(C,\delta)
  \le
  \frac{
    n^{1+o(1)}
    +2^{(\beta(\rho)/\Htwo(\rho))\QH(\eta)(1+o(1))}
  }{\qline},
\]
or the same bound over \(\qgen\) plus a proved extension-line lift.  If the
consumed primitive is line decoding, then
Proposition~\ref{prop:list-mca-to-line-decoding-bridge} should give
\[
  C\text{ is }(\delta,\ M+nL+1,\ n+1)\text{ line-decodable},
\]
with \(M\) and \(L\) supplied by M1 and L1.
\end{conjecture}

This package would turn the current negative cap into a sharp threshold program:
below the cap one cannot hope for \(2^{-128}\) MCA soundness, while above the
corrected reserve one would have finite numerators that can be checked against
\(\qline\).

\section{Fresh Non-Claims}
\label{sec:fresh-nonclaims}

This revision does not claim an exact settlement of the smooth-case Proximity
Prize threshold.  It claims that the shape is now rigid:
\[
  \boxed{\text{No unslacked capacity theorem; prove a reserve theorem at }
  1-\rho-\eta.}
\]
The theorem-backed negative side gives a draft universal MCA cap at constant gap
\(2^{-9}\) for \(\rho=1/2,1/4,1/8\) and \(2^{-10}\) for \(\rho=1/16\).  The
positive side should be pursued as
\[
  \text{L1 locator local limit}
  +\text{M1 residue-line packing}
  +\text{extension-line theorem}
  +\text{protocol-ledger rewrite}.
\]
The interleaved-list problem is less fundamental than it looked in the old file:
for column-distance interleaving, a uniform locator-fiber theorem immediately
bounds the interleaved list at the same exponent.

\section{BCHKS Locator Construction in Smooth-Quotient Notation}
\label{sec:bchks}

\textbf{Status: \textsc{Imported} plus \textsc{Wrapper}.}
This section is a provenance-safe restatement and dictionary.  It is included
so later main-paper edits have a clean place to copy from.

\subsection{Imported theorem/proof pattern}

BCHKS prove a general multiplicative-subgroup construction
\cite[Thm.~7.1]{BCHKS25}.  In a compact form, the imported statement is as
follows.

\begin{theorem}[BCHKS quotient-locator construction, imported]
\label{thm:bchks-import}
Let \(G\subseteq H\subseteq \F_q^*\) be multiplicative subgroups and let
\(\Phi:H\to G\) be \(x\mapsto x^c\), where \(c=|H|/|G|\).  Let
\(E\subseteq G\), and let \(\ellpg,a\geq1\) satisfy
\[
  |E^{(+\ellpg)}|
  =\#\{e_1+\cdots+e_{\ellpg}:e_i\in E\text{ distinct}\}\geq a.
\]
Let \(D=\Phi^{-1}(E)\), \(n=|D|\), and
\[
  C=\RS[\F_q,D,n-(\ellpg+2)c].
\]
Then, for \(\gamma=\ellpg c/n\), there are functions
\(f,g:D\to\F_q\) such that
\[
  \#\{z\in\F_q: \dist(f+zg,C)\leq \gamma\}\geq a,
\]
while
\[
  \dist([f,g],C^2)\geq (\ellpg+1)c/n.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Reference]
This is exactly the imported construction of \cite[Thm.~7.1]{BCHKS25}; the
proof is not reproduced as a repository contribution.  BCHKS define the line
endpoints by the monomials \(x^{n-\ellpg c}\) and
\(x^{n-(\ellpg+1)c}\), then expand a vanishing polynomial over the complement
of a chosen \(\ellpg\)-subset.  The coefficient of
\(X^{n-(\ellpg+1)c}\) is an affine transform of a restricted sum from
\(E^{(+\ellpg)}\), giving the exceptional slope values.
\end{proof}

The BCHKS Theorem~1.13 is the admissible-subgroup specialization of this
construction: take \(E=G\), take \(H=D\), set \(c=|D|/|G|\), and use
\(\ellpg=|G|/2\).  Any RS-MCA statement corresponding to that specialization
should cite \cite[Thm.~1.13]{BCHKS25} directly.

\begin{lemma}[Locator expansion, imported identity]

\subsection{List-fiber pigeonhole wrapper}

The next proposition is the repository-side wrapper around the imported
identity.  It is intentionally stated in certificate form, because distinctness
of the nearby codewords is a separate check in concrete instantiations.

\begin{proposition}[List-fiber pigeonhole wrapper]
\label{prop:list-fiber-wrapper}
Let \(\mathcal A\) be a family of \(\ellpg\)-subsets of a quotient set \(E\).
Suppose the BCHKS locator construction assigns to each \(A\in\mathcal A\) a
slope \(z(A)\) lying in a finite set \(S\), and a locator certificate showing
that the corresponding word \(f+z(A)g\) is within radius \(\gamma\) of a
locator codeword \(P_A\in C\).  Then some slope \(z_0\in S\) has at least
\[
  \frac{|\mathcal A|}{|S|}
\]
locator certificates above it.  If the codewords \(P_A\) attached to the
certificates over \(z_0\) are distinct, then the Hamming ball of radius
\(\gamma\) around \(f+z_0g\) contains at least \(|\mathcal A|/|S|\) codewords
of \(C\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the pigeonhole principle applied to the map
\(A\mapsto z(A)\).  The second assertion is only the definition of list size,
after the distinctness check removes possible duplicate locator codewords.

Theorem~\ref{thm:bchks-import}.
\end{proof}

extra bookkeeping needed by RS-MCA: which slope set pays the pigeonhole bill,

Those are the checks needed before using the construction in Papers A, B, or D.
\end{remark}

\section{Cycle 14 Normal Form}

\begin{lemma}[Affine wedge normal form]
\label{lem:affine-wedge}
Assume \(\{[W]_E,b\}\) is an \(F\)-basis of \(A\).  Then the landing equation can
be written in the form
\[
  \iota(\tau)=A_0(\tauone,\tautwo)-\tauthree [W]_E,

\]
where
\[
  A_0=p_1[W]_E+p_2 b,
  \qquad
  B_0=q_1[W]_E+q_2 b,
\]
and \(p_i,q_i\in F\) are affine-linear functions of
\((\tauone,\tautwo)\).  If the basis is normalized so \([W]_E\wedge b=1\), then
\[
  \Delta=\iota\wedge\mu
    =(p_1-\tauthree)(q_2-\tauthree)-p_2q_1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Cycle 14 quotient calculation, which is a concrete instance of Paper B's
residue-line normal form, gives the displayed affine forms.  Expanding in the

\((q_1,q_2-\tauthree)\).  Their wedge determinant is therefore
\[
  (p_1-\tauthree)(q_2-\tauthree)-p_2q_1.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Cycle 18 Component Split}

\begin{theorem}[Monic quadratic and linear alpha component]
\label{thm:component-split}
In the setting above,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Deltazero={}&
  \tauthree^2-(p_{10}+q_{20})\tauthree
  +p_{10}q_{20}+\nu p_{11}q_{21}
  -p_{20}q_{10}-\nu p_{21}q_{11},\\
\Deltaone={}&
  -(p_{11}+q_{21})\tauthree
  +p_{10}q_{21}+p_{11}q_{20}-p_{20}q_{11}-p_{21}q_{10}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently \(\Deltazero\) is monic quadratic in \(\tauthree\), while
\(\Deltaone\) has degree at most one in \(\tauthree\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the coordinate expressions for \(p_i,q_i\) into the identity of
Lemma~\ref{lem:affine-wedge} and use \(\alpha^2=\nu\).  Collecting the
coefficient of \(1\) gives the displayed formula for \(\Deltazero\), and
collecting the coefficient of \(\alpha\) gives the displayed formula for
\(\Deltaone\).  The coefficient of \(\tauthree^2\) in \(\Deltazero\) is \(1\),
and the \(\alpha\)-component has no \(\tauthree^2\)-term.
\end{proof}

\section{New Divisibility-Gate Theorem}
\label{sec:divisibility-gate}

The next theorem is the main \textsc{New-local} theorem extracted from the
Cycle 18 reduction.  It is stronger than merely saying that the branch is a
graph: it shows that any two-dimensional family must satisfy an explicit
divisibility identity.

\begin{theorem}[Divisibility gate for the graph branch]
\label{thm:divisibility-gate}
Assume the restricted setting above and assume \(\Deltaone\ne0\).  Write
\[
  \Deltaone=s(\tauone,\tautwo)\tauthree+h(\tauone,\tautwo),
\]
and define the denominator-cleared graph polynomial
\[
  G(\tauone,\tautwo)
  =s(\tauone,\tautwo)^2
    \Deltazero\bigl(\tauone,\tautwo,-h(\tauone,\tautwo)/s(\tauone,\tautwo)\bigr).
\]
Then \(\deg G\le 4\).  If \(G\) is not the zero polynomial, then
\[
  \#\{\,\tau\in B^3:\Deltazero(\tau)=\Deltaone(\tau)=0\,\}=O(p).
\]
Consequently the corresponding number of distinct slopes satisfies
\[
  \Ctwo=O(p)=O(n).
\]
Therefore a growing-prime family with
\[
  \Ctwo=\Theta(p^2)=\Theta(\qline)
\]
in the nonzero-\(\Deltaone\) branch can occur only if \(G\equiv0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because the \(p_i,q_i\) are affine-linear in \((\tauone,\tautwo)\), the
coefficient \(s\) has degree at most \(1\), and \(h\) has degree at most \(2\).
Since \(\Deltazero\) is monic quadratic in \(\tauthree\), clearing the
denominators after substituting \(\tauthree=-h/s\) gives a polynomial \(G\) of
degree at most \(4\).

On \(s\ne0\), the equation \(\Deltaone=0\) forces \(\tauthree=-h/s\).  Hence
every common zero on this open locus gives a base pair
\((\tauone,\tautwo)\) with \(G(\tauone,\tautwo)=0\).  If \(G\) is nonzero,
Schwartz--Zippel gives at most \(4p\) such base pairs, and each base pair gives
at most one value of \(\tauthree\).

It remains to count the exceptional locus \(s=0\).  There \(\Deltaone=0\) also
forces \(h=0\).  If \(s\) is a nonzero affine form, this is contained in an
affine line; if \(s\equiv0\), then \(h\) is a nonzero polynomial of degree at
most \(2\).  In either case there are \(O(p)\) base pairs.  For each such pair,
\(\Deltazero\) is a monic quadratic in \(\tauthree\), so it gives at most two
values of \(\tauthree\).  The total common-zero count is \(O(p)\).

Finally, the number of distinct slopes is bounded by the number of landing
points \(\tau\).  Thus \(\Ctwo=O(p)\) whenever \(G\not\equiv0\), and any
\(\Theta(p^2)\) family in this branch must satisfy \(G\equiv0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The identity \(G\equiv0\) says that, after clearing the \(s\)-denominator,
\(\Deltazero\) is divisible by the linear equation \(\Deltaone\) on the graph
branch.  This is the first exact algebraic gate that a large slope family in
this restricted window must pass.
\end{remark}

\section{Slope Map and Remaining Heuristics}
\label{sec:slope-map}

On the graph branch, the landing equation \(\iota=z\mu\) gives
\[
  p_1-\tauthree=zq_1,
  \qquad
  p_2=z(q_2-\tauthree).
\]
Where \(q_1\ne0\), substituting \(\tauthree=-h/s\) yields
\[
  z(\tauone,\tautwo)=\frac{p_1+h/s}{q_1}.
\]
Equivalently, eliminating \(\tauthree\) recovers the Cycle 14 quadratic
\[
  q_1z^2-(p_1-q_2)z-p_2=0,
\]
so the residual map is controlled by
\[
  (\tauone,\tautwo)
  \longmapsto [\,q_1:(p_1-q_2):p_2\,]\in\mathbb P^2(F).
\]

\begin{heuristic}[Exceptional identity should be rare]
For generic source data, the polynomial \(G\) should not vanish identically.
Thus the graph branch should usually be curve-sized, and the first possible
large-slope families should come from algebraically special data satisfying
the divisibility identity \(G\equiv0\) or from the zero-alpha branch
\(\Deltaone\equiv0\).
\end{heuristic}

\begin{heuristic}[What remains after the gate]
If \(G\equiv0\), the problem is no longer to show that the common-zero set is
small.  It may be surface-sized.  The remaining question is whether the
projective coefficient map has one-dimensional image on every source-valid
surface, giving \(\Ctwo=O(p)\), or whether some growing-prime source-valid
family has two-dimensional image and \(\Ctwo=\Theta(p^2)\).
\end{heuristic}

\subsection{Syndrome-pencil normal form for support-wise line incidence}

\textbf{Status: \textsc{New-local}.}
This is the cleanest reusable F1 contribution from the batch.  It turns
support-wise line incidence into a Hankel-pencil locator equation over the
actual line field.

Let \(D=\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\subset F\) have distinct points and let
\(C=\RS[F,D,k]\) with redundancy \(r=n-k\).  For each \(x_i\), put
\[
  \lambda_i=\left(\prod_{h\ne i}(x_i-x_h)\right)^{-1}.
\]
For a word \(y:D\to F\), define
\[
  \operatorname{Syn}(y)_m
  =\sum_i \lambda_i x_i^m y(x_i),
  \qquad 0\le m<r.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{Syn}(y)=0\) if and only if \(y\in C\).  Fix
\(T\subset D\) with \(|T|=j\), assume \(0\le j\le r\), and set \(t=r-j\).
Write
\[
  L_T(X)=\prod_{x\in T}(X-x)=\ell_0+\ell_1X+\cdots+\ell_jX^j,
  \qquad
  \boldsymbol\ell_T=(\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_j)^T .
\]
For \(w\in F^r\), define the \(t\times(j+1)\) Hankel window
\[
  H_{t,j}(w)_{a,b}=w_{a+b},
  \qquad 0\le a<t,\quad 0\le b\le j.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Hankel-pencil test]
\label{thm:syndrome-pencil}
Let \(f,g:D\to F\), put \(u=\operatorname{Syn}(f)\) and
\(v=\operatorname{Syn}(g)\), and let \(S=D\setminus T\).  For a finite slope
\(z\in F\), the word \(f+zg\) is explained by a codeword of \(C\) on \(S\) if
and only if
\[
  \bigl(H_{t,j}(u)+zH_{t,j}(v)\bigr)\boldsymbol\ell_T=0.
\]
Moreover, this explanation is support-wise noncontained for the line
\(f+zg\) on \(S\) if and only if, in addition,
\[
  H_{t,j}(v)\boldsymbol\ell_T\ne0.
\]
Consequently the support-wise MCA bad slopes at agreement \(k+t\) are exactly
the slopes for which there is a squarefree \(D\)-split locator \(L_T\) of
degree \(j=r-t\) satisfying the displayed incidence and noncontainment tests.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(W_T\subset F^r\) be the span of the parity-check columns indexed by
\(T\):
\[
  W_T=\operatorname{span}_F\{\lambda_x(1,x,\ldots,x^{r-1}):x\in T\}.
\]
Equivalently, \(w\in W_T\) means that \(w\) is the syndrome of a word supported
on \(T\).  For every \(w\in W_T\), write
\[
  w_m=\sum_{x\in T}c_xx^m.
\]
Then, for \(0\le a<t\),
\[
  \bigl(H_{t,j}(w)\boldsymbol\ell_T\bigr)_a
  =\sum_{b=0}^j\ell_b w_{a+b}
  =\sum_{x\in T}c_xx^aL_T(x)=0.
\]
Conversely, since \(L_T\) is monic, the recurrence
\(\sum_{b=0}^j\ell_b w_{a+b}=0\) for \(0\le a<r-j\) determines all remaining
coordinates from the first \(j\) coordinates, so its solution space has
dimension at most \(j\).  The \(j\) columns indexed by the distinct points of
\(T\) form a Vandermonde system of rank \(j\), hence the recurrence space is
exactly \(W_T\).

The word \(f+zg\) is explained on \(S\) if and only if there is \(c\in C\) such
that \(f+zg-c\) is supported on \(T\).  Taking syndromes, this is equivalent to
\(u+zv\in W_T\), which is the first displayed equation by the recurrence
criterion.  Under that equation, simultaneous explanation of \(f\) and \(g\) on
the same \(S\) is equivalent to \(u,v\in W_T\).  Since
\(u=(u+zv)-zv\), this is equivalent to \(v\in W_T\), hence to
\(H_{t,j}(v)\boldsymbol\ell_T=0\).  Noncontainment is therefore exactly the
negation of that condition.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Interleaved codegree reduction}

\textbf{Status: \textsc{New-local} reduction; saving remains conditional.}
This result is useful for the L2 interleaved-list ledger because it replaces
the naive Cartesian product by a recursion through higher-agreement tails.

Let \(C=\RS[F,D,k]\).  For a word \(V:D\to F\) and codeword \(c\in C\), write
\[
  A_V(c)=\{x\in D:c(x)=V(x)\},
  \qquad
  \operatorname{Fib}_V(s)=\{c\in C:|A_V(c)|\ge s\},
\]
and \(M_V(s)=|\operatorname{Fib}_V(s)|\).  For a \(\mu\)-row word
\(U=(U_1,\ldots,U_\mu)\), define
\[
  \Lambda_\mu^{(s)}(U)
  =
  \#\{(c_1,\ldots,c_\mu)\in C^\mu:
  |\cap_i A_{U_i}(c_i)|\ge s\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[two-regime codegree bound]
\label{thm:l2-codegree}
For every agreement threshold \(a\),
\[
  \Lambda_2^{(a)}(U_1,U_2)
  \le
  M_{U_2}(a)+M_{U_2}(2a-k)\,M_{U_1}(a).
\]
More generally,
\[
  \Lambda_\mu^{(a)}(U_1,\ldots,U_\mu)
  \le
  \Lambda_{\mu-1}^{(a)}(U_2,\ldots,U_\mu)
  +
  \Lambda_{\mu-1}^{(2a-k)}(U_2,\ldots,U_\mu)\,M_{U_1}(a).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the two-row case, sum over \(c_2\in\operatorname{Fib}_{U_2}(a)\), and put
\(N_2=|A_{U_2}(c_2)|\).  The possible \(c_1\)'s are codewords agreeing with
\(U_1\) on at least \(a\) points of \(A_{U_2}(c_2)\).  If
\(N_2<2a-k\), then two such \(c_1\)'s would agree with each other on more than
\(k\) points, forcing equality.  Thus this punctured list has size at most
one.  If \(N_2\ge2a-k\), use the trivial bound \(M_{U_1}(a)\).  Summing these
two regimes gives the displayed two-row inequality.

For the \(\mu\)-row case, fix \((c_2,\ldots,c_\mu)\) and put
\[
  S=\bigcap_{i=2}^\mu A_{U_i}(c_i).
\]
The listed \(c_1\)'s must agree with \(U_1\) on at least \(a\) points of \(S\).
If \(|S|<2a-k\), the same unique-decoding argument gives at most one such
\(c_1\).  If \(|S|\ge2a-k\), use the bound \(M_{U_1}(a)\).  Counting the two
classes of \((c_2,\ldots,c_\mu)\) gives the recursion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The reduction is unconditional.  The desired L2 saving still requires an
L1-family input saying that the tail lists at agreement \(2a-k\) are small
after quotient-periodic contributions are budgeted.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Deep-point cap dependency split}

\textbf{Status: \textsc{Wrapper} / \textsc{Audit}.}
This result does not edit Paper D, but it explains why the headline MCA cap can
be routed locally without using the imported Crites--Stewart conversion.

\begin{proposition}[CS25-free route to the Paper D MCA cap]
\label{prop:deep-point-cap}
Assume the finite-field, coset, divisibility, and binomial hypotheses used in
Paper D's universal cap theorem.  Let \(C=\RS[F,D,k]\), \(C^+=\RS[F,D,k+1]\),
\(q=|F|\), and suppose the elementary quotient-locator fiber construction gives
a \(C^+\)-list of size
\[
  L\ge \binom{N}{\rho N+2}/|B|\ge q/k+1
\]
at the endpoint \(\delta_N=1-\rho-2/N\).  Then there is a support-wise MCA-bad
line for \(C\) with bad-slope density strictly larger than
\[
  \frac1{2k}\left(1-\frac nq\right).
\]
By support-wise MCA monotonicity, the same lower bound holds for all
\(\delta_N\le\delta<1-\rho\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(U:D\to F\) be the received word with a \(C^+\)-list
\(\{P_1,\ldots,P_L\}\) at agreement \(m=k+2n/N\).  For
\(\alpha\in F\setminus D\), form
\[
  f_\alpha(x)=\frac{U(x)}{x-\alpha},
  \qquad
  g_\alpha(x)=-\frac1{x-\alpha}.
\]
For every listed \(P_i\), the slope \(z=P_i(\alpha)\) gives the codeword
\[
  Q_i(X)=\frac{P_i(X)-P_i(\alpha)}{X-\alpha}\in F[X]_{<k}
\]
on the same agreement support.  Conversely every such slope comes from a
listed \(P_i\).  Since \(m>k\), the direction \(g_\alpha\) cannot be explained
by a degree-\(<k\) polynomial on the same support, so these slopes are
support-wise MCA-bad.

Averaging over \(\Omega=F\setminus D\), two distinct degree-\(<k+1\)
polynomials collide at at most \(k\) points of \(\Omega\).  Therefore some
\(\alpha\) has at least
\[
  M\ge \frac{L}{1+k(L-1)/(q-n)}
\]
distinct values among \(P_1(\alpha),\ldots,P_L(\alpha)\).  Its bad-slope
density is at least
\[
  \frac{L(q-n)}{q(q-n+k(L-1))}.
\]
This is strictly greater than \((q-n)/(2kq)\) exactly when
\[
  2kL>q-n+k(L-1).
\]
The lower bound \(L\ge q/k+1\) gives \(kL\ge q+k\), so the left-minus-right
quantity is at least \(n+2k>0\).  This proves the endpoint bound, and
monotonicity in \(\delta\) extends it to the stated interval.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Common code-line residual budget}

\textbf{Status: \textsc{New-local} finite MDS theorem.}
This M2 lemma is a guardrail for line-decoding imports: a close common
code-line exception still pays for residual coordinates outside the common
support.

\begin{proposition}[common code-line residual budget]
\label{prop:common-residual}
Let \(C\subseteq F^D\) be an MDS linear code of dimension \(k\), so every
nonzero codeword has fewer than \(k\) zeros on \(D\).  Let
\(\ell_z=f+zg\) be a received line and suppose that for some codewords
\(c_f,c_g\in C\) there is a common support \(S_0\subseteq D\) of size \(b\)
such that \(f=c_f\) and \(g=c_g\) on \(S_0\).  Fix an agreement threshold
\(a\) with \(a+b-n\ge k\).  Put \(\Omega=D\setminus S_0\),
\[
  f'=f-c_f,\qquad g'=g-c_g,\qquad
  h=\max(1,a-b),
\]
and
\[
  c_0=\#\{x\in\Omega:f'(x)=g'(x)=0\}.
\]
Every support-wise noncontained slope at agreement \(a\) satisfies
\[
  \#\{x\in\Omega:f'(x)+zg'(x)=0\}\ge h.
\]
Consequently, if \(h>c_0\), then the number of such slopes is at most
\[
  \left\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|-c_0}{h-c_0}\right\rfloor.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(\ell_z\) is explained on a support \(S\) of size at least \(a\), then
\[
  |S\cap S_0|\ge a-|\Omega|=a+b-n\ge k.
\]
On \(S\cap S_0\), the explaining codeword agrees with \(c_f+zc_g\).  By the
MDS zero-rigidity condition, the two codewords are equal.  Thus, outside
\(S_0\), the line can only be explained where \(f'+zg'=0\), and at least
\(h=\max(1,a-b)\) such outside zeros are needed for a noncontained support.

Each non-common residual coordinate \(x\in\Omega\) with \((f'(x),g'(x))\ne
(0,0)\) can vanish for at most one finite slope \(z\).  A bad slope needs at
least \(h-c_0\) private non-common residual zeros after the \(c_0\) common zeros
are counted.  Charging these private coordinates to slopes gives the stated
packing bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{M1: variable-line packet reduction}

The follow-up variable-line packet note isolates a local reduction inside the
same Hankel-pencil branch.  Fix a \((j-2)\)-core \(R\) and a non-fixed proper
one-dimensional determinant component \(L\) in the two-root plane
\[
  T=R\cup\{x,y\},\qquad s=x+y,\quad p=xy .
\]
Under the stated packet hypotheses, the active-new mass \(r_L\) on \(L\) obeys
\[
  r_L
  \le
  {\bf 1}_{d_L=m_L=r_L=1}
  +(d_L-m_L)+2\binom{m_L}{2}.
\]
The unordered packet pairs in the final term inject into the global
different-slope two-exchange codegree ledger, so summing over non-fixed
variable lines gives
\[
  \sum_L r_L
  \le S_{\rm dom}+Q_{\rm def}+2E_{\rm pkt}.
\]
The remaining domain-singleton term is reduced to contained/tangent and
one-outside target images:
\[
  S_{\rm dom}
  \le
  Z_0
  +2\binom{j}{2}\binom{n-j}{2}|{\rm Target}_{\rm cb}|
  +(j-1)\binom{n-j+1}{2}|{\rm Target}_{\rm off}|.
\]
Writing \(a=n-j\), the exceptional zero-lower term vanishes whenever
\[
  a>\frac{n+1}{2},
\]
and hence throughout the positive-slack rate-half range \(k\ge n/2\),
\(a=k+t\), \(t\ge1\).  Consequently the non-fixed variable-line branch is
reduced to two explicit residual estimates: the active different-slope
two-exchange codegree and the one-outside boundary target image, after quotient
defects and contained/tangent ledgers have been paid.

The boundary-off external-anchor note gives a further local normal form for
the target image.  Writing a one-outside target as \(B=S\cup\{\beta\}\),
\(|S|=j-1\), the locator vector is
\[
  \ell_{S,\beta}=\ell_S^+-\beta\ell_S^0,
\]
so the Hankel landing condition is a one-variable external-anchor gate
\[
  \operatorname{rank}
  \bigl[
    A_u(S)-\beta B_u(S)\quad A_v(S)-\beta B_v(S)
  \bigr]\le 1.
\]
All \(2\times2\) minors are quadratic in \(\beta\).  Hence, for a fixed domain
shadow \(S\), either there are at most two external anchors, or the whole
external-anchor pencil is a ruled rank-one artifact with a common image line
or a common kernel line.  This still does not bound
\({\rm Target}_{\rm off}\), but it replaces the boundary residual by a precise
quadratic-anchor/ruled-branch incidence target.

The quadratic minors have an explicit coefficient ledger.  If
\[
  A=H(u)\ell_S^+,\quad B=H(u)\ell_S^0,\quad
  C=H(v)\ell_S^+,\quad D=H(v)\ell_S^0,
\]
then each row-pair minor is
\[
  W(A,C)-\beta\{W(B,C)+W(A,D)\}+\beta^2 W(B,D).
\]
Thus a ruled shadow is detected by three Hankel-wedge coefficient families,
and in the \(t=2\) case by three scalar equations.

The nondegenerate part is therefore bounded by twice the first boundary-shadow
image of the active locator family.  Its popular part is already an
active-exchange ledger: if a \((j-1)\)-shadow is adjacent to two active
all-domain locators, then those locators have exchange distance at most \(3\).
More precisely,
\[
|\mathrm{Boundary}_{\mathrm{off}}^{\mathrm{quad}}|
 \le 2|\mathrm{Iso}_{\mathrm{quad}}|
  +2(N_1E_1+N_2E_2+N_3E_3),
\]
where \(\mathrm{Iso}_{\mathrm{quad}}\) is the unique-neighbor nondegenerate
shadow ledger and \(E_h\) are the first three active exchange profiles.  In the
opposite direction, this residual is sharp for exchange bookkeeping: if
\(E_1=E_2=E_3=0\), then every first boundary shadow is unique and
\[
|\mathrm{Shadow}_1(A)|=|A|\binom{j}{2}(n-j).
\]
So the unique-neighbor ledger cannot be removed by low-exchange estimates
alone; it needs Hankel, quotient, aperiodic, or isolated-active-locator input.
One immediate Hankel cleanup is available: for a fixed external anchor
\(\beta\), each row-pair anchor minor is a quadratic polynomial on monic
degree-\((j-1)\) shadow locators.  If such a minor vanishes on the full shadow
star through \(\alpha\in D\), and \(|D\setminus\{\alpha\}|\ge j\), then it
vanishes on the whole hyperplane \(L_S(\alpha)=0\).  Equivalently the minor has
the evaluation factor \(L_S(\alpha)\), so full anchor stars are
fixed-root/root-slice charges.  The remaining nondegenerate unique-neighbor
residual is therefore star-free after those charges.  Since the minor is
quadratic, two distinct full stars force the exact product
\(cL_S(\alpha_1)L_S(\alpha_2)\), and three distinct full stars force the minor
to vanish identically.  The finite interpolation input is the quadratic
cofactor Vandermonde lemma: degree-\(\le2\) functions on monic degree-\(r\)
locators are determined by the degree-\(r\) cofactors of any \((r+2)\)-set.
There is also a complementary core-line bound for each fixed anchor minor:
after charging the \((j-2)\)-cores \(R\) for which
\(x\mapsto P(L_{R\cup\{x\}})\) vanishes identically, the remaining split zeros
of that minor are bounded by
\[
  \frac{2}{j-1}\binom{n}{j-2}.
\]
This is still a per-minor statement, but it turns the star-free fixed-anchor
zero set into a one-root-loss ledger plus explicit core-line degeneracies.  The
degeneracies themselves have the same coefficient-ledger form one level lower:
for fixed \(R\), write
\[
  (X-\beta)(X-Y)L_R=(X-\beta)X L_R-Y(X-\beta)L_R.
\]
If \(A,C\) are the \(H(u),H(v)\) images of \((X-\beta)X L_R\), and \(B,D\) are
the corresponding images of \((X-\beta)L_R\), then the restricted minor is
\[
  W(A,C)-Y\{W(B,C)+W(A,D)\}+Y^2W(B,D).
\]
Thus an identically vanishing core-line is exactly the simultaneous vanishing
of these three lower-dimensional Hankel-wedge coefficients.  If the full
rank-one anchor gate, rather than one row-pair minor, vanishes identically on
the core-line, then the linear Hankel pair is already ruled one level lower:
with \(A,B,C,D\) as above, either \(A=B=0\) or \(C=D=0\), or
\(\mathrm{span}\{A,B,C,D\}\) is a fixed image line, or
\(\mathrm{span}\{A,B\}\) has dimension \(2\) and \(C=\lambda A\),
\(D=\lambda B\).  In the last case a fixed projective column-kernel line kills
both lower anchor shifts \((X-\beta)XL_R\) and \((X-\beta)L_R\).  This also
upgrades the per-minor core-line bound to the full rank-one gate: for fixed
\(\beta\), after charging the cores on which the whole gate is identically
rank one, every remaining \((j-2)\)-core has at most two rank-one extensions.
Hence the uncharged full-gate shadows for that anchor are bounded by
\[
  \frac{2}{j-1}\binom{n}{j-2}.
\]
The charged full core-lines also have explicit row-rank ledgers one level
lower.  On monic degree-\((j-2)\) locators \(L_R\), with
\(M_0=(X-\beta)L_R\) and \(M_1=(X-\beta)XL_R\), they are contained in the
endpoint systems \(H(u)M_i=0\) or \(H(v)M_i=0\), the fixed-kernel systems
\((H(v)-\lambda H(u))M_i=0\), or the projective image-line systems
\(y\wedge H(w)M_i=0\) for \(w\in\{u,v\}\) and \(i=0,1\).  If all uncharged
lower systems have direction dimension at most \(b\), then for fixed
\(\beta\)
\[
|\mathrm{FullCoreGate}(\beta)|
 \le \left(2+q+\frac{q^t-1}{q-1}\right)\binom{n}{b},
\]
and their shadow contribution pays only the extra extension factor \(n-j+2\).
The lower fixed-kernel slopes obey the same finite-slope dichotomy as the
original ruled branch: if \(c=j-2\) and \(0\le b<c\), then for fixed
\(\beta\) either there are at most \(c-b\) slopes \(\lambda\) with lower core
direction dimension \(>b\), or the lower core pencil
\((H(v)-\lambda H(u))(X-\beta)L_R\) and
\((H(v)-\lambda H(u))(X-\beta)XL_R\) is persistently low-rank for every
\(\lambda\).  Away from those exceptional slopes, the fixed-kernel full-core
ledger contributes at most \(q\binom{n}{b}\) cores, up to the same extension
factor for shadows.
The persistent lower-kernel alternative is again not a new free obstruction:
writing the lower pencil as \(R_v^{\rm core}-\lambda R_u^{\rm core}\), vanishing
of all \((c-b)\times(c-b)\) minors forces both endpoint core systems to have
direction dimension \(>b\).  Equivalently, if either lower endpoint rank is
good, the finite-exception alternative applies.  In the persistent case there
are moving core directions \(Q(\lambda)\) killed by both lower shifts, with
coefficient ladder
\[
R_v^{\rm core}q_0=0,\qquad
R_v^{\rm core}q_i-R_u^{\rm core}q_{i-1}=0,\qquad
R_u^{\rm core}q_D=0.
\]
The lower common-image ledger has the matching projective-determinantal form.
For \(I=[y]\), the direction map
\[
Q\longmapsto
\bigl(y\wedge H(u)(X-\beta)Q,\,
      y\wedge H(u)(X-\beta)XQ,\,
      y\wedge H(v)(X-\beta)Q,\,
      y\wedge H(v)(X-\beta)XQ\bigr)
\]
has kernel dimension \(e_I(\beta)\).  The locus \(e_I(\beta)>b\) is cut out by
homogeneous \((c-b)\times(c-b)\) minors in projective \(y\); if it is not
identically persistent it has at most \((c-b)q^{t-1}/(q-1)\) projective lines.
Outside it, the lower common-image full-core ledger contributes at most
\(((q^t-1)/(q-1))\binom{n}{b}\) cores before the same extension factor.
Combining these lower ledgers gives a fixed-anchor closure criterion: after
charging lower endpoint systems with dimension \(>b\), lower fixed-kernel
slopes with dimension \(>b\), projective image lines with dimension \(>b\), and
the persistent moving-certificate ledgers, the remaining full-gate shadows for
that anchor are bounded by
\[
  \frac{2}{j-1}\binom{n}{j-2}
  +(n-j+2)\left(2+q+\frac{q^t-1}{q-1}\right)\binom{n}{b}.
\]
This is a fixed-anchor closure statement for the full-core ruled pieces; the
one-root-loss residual remains a separate boundary-shadow obligation.
There is also a root-free refinement of the bounded-rank full-core term.  After
common-root lower core pieces have been charged, a lower affine row-rank
stratum of degree \(c=j-2\) and direction dimension \(e\ge1\) contributes at
most
\[
  \frac{e}{c}\binom{n}{e}
\]
split cores, rather than \(\binom{n}{e}\).  Thus in the range
\(1\le b\le n/2\), the bounded-rank term in the fixed-anchor closure gains a
factor \(b/c\); the one-root-loss residual is unchanged.
The endpoint charges themselves also have an anchor dichotomy.  For
\(w\in\{u,v\}\), the lower endpoint direction matrix is affine in \(\beta\):
\[
R_w^{\rm core}(\beta)=R_{w,+}^{\rm core}-\beta R_{w,0}^{\rm core},
\]
corresponding to the two shifted systems
\((H(w)XQ,H(w)X^2Q)\) and \((H(w)Q,H(w)XQ)\).  Hence either at most \(c-b\)
anchors have endpoint core direction dimension \(>b\), or the endpoint-anchor
pencil is persistently low-rank for every \(\beta\), with moving endpoint-core
certificates over \(F(\beta)\).
The finite fixed-kernel charges can also be made global in the anchor--slope
plane.  For a pair \((\beta,\lambda)\), the lower direction matrix for
\[
(H(v)-\lambda H(u))(X-\beta)Q=0,\qquad
(H(v)-\lambda H(u))(X-\beta)XQ=0
\]
has entries of bidegree at most \((1,1)\) in \((\beta,\lambda)\).  Therefore
the locus where its core direction dimension is \(>b\) is cut out by
\((c-b)\times(c-b)\) minors of total degree at most \(2(c-b)\).  If this
determinantal locus is not identically persistent, it contains at most
\(2(c-b)q\) anchor--slope pairs; otherwise it gives moving two-parameter core
certificates over \(F(\beta,\lambda)\).
The common-image charges have the analogous global incidence form over
\(F\times \mathbb P^{t-1}\).  For \((\beta,[y])\), the map
\[
Q\longmapsto
\bigl(y\wedge H(u)(X-\beta)Q,\,
      y\wedge H(u)(X-\beta)XQ,\,
      y\wedge H(v)(X-\beta)Q,\,
      y\wedge H(v)(X-\beta)XQ\bigr)
\]
has entries of degree at most one in \(\beta\) and homogeneous degree one in
\(y\).  Hence the bad incidence where the core direction dimension is \(>b\) is
cut out by minors of total degree at most \(2(c-b)\).  If this incidence is not
identically persistent, it has at most \(2(c-b)q^t/(q-1)\) rational
anchor--image pairs; otherwise it gives moving anchor--image core certificates
over \(F(\beta,y)\).
Together these global charges give an all-anchor full-gate incidence closure.
After endpoint-bad anchors, bad anchor--slope pairs, bad anchor--image
incidences, persistent moving-certificate ledgers, and common-root lower core
pieces are charged, the remaining root-free all-anchor incidence satisfies
\[
|\mathcal Z_{\rm all}^{\rm rf,\le b}|
 \le q\,\frac{2}{m}\binom{n}{m-1}
 +q(n-m+1)\left(2+q+\frac{q^t-1}{q-1}\right)
    \frac{b}{c}\binom{n}{b}.
\]
Without the common-root/root-free charge the same bound holds with the factor
\(b/c\) removed from the second term.  Projection to the anchor coordinate can
only decrease this count, so this is also a conditional all-anchor support
bound for the uncharged full-core component.
There is also a direct anchor-multiplicity alternative for the full core-lines
themselves.  For a fixed \((m-1)\)-core \(R\), consider
\[
N_R(\beta,Y)=
\bigl[H(u)(X-\beta)(X-Y)L_R\quad H(v)(X-\beta)(X-Y)L_R\bigr].
\]
If the rank-one condition for \(N_R(\beta,Y)\) is not an identity in both
\(\beta\) and \(Y\), then the set of anchors \(\beta\) for which the whole
core-line is full has size at most \(2\).  Hence, after globally full
two-variable core-lines are charged, the all-anchor full-core incidence is at
most \(2(n-m+1)\binom{n}{m-1}\).  Together with the Corollary 25 residual this
gives the direct bound
\[
\left(\frac{2q}{m}+2(n-m+1)\right)\binom{n}{m-1}
\]
for all rank-one anchor--shadow incidences with no globally full core.
The globally full core-lines themselves have the same ruled shape one shift
lower.  Writing
\[
U_i=H(u)X^iL_R,\qquad V_i=H(v)X^iL_R,\qquad i=0,1,2,
\]
the identity \(\operatorname{rank}N_R(\beta,Y)\le1\) for all \((\beta,Y)\)
forces either \(U_0=U_1=U_2=0\), or \(V_0=V_1=V_2=0\), or all six vectors
span one image line, or \(V_i=\lambda U_i\) for \(i=0,1,2\).  Thus the
globally full obstruction is a three-shift endpoint/common-image/fixed-kernel
row-rank ledger.  If the uncharged three-shift direction dimensions are at most
\(b\), then
\[
|\mathrm{GlobalFullCore}^{\le b}|
 \le \left(2+q+\frac{q^t-1}{q-1}\right)\binom{n}{b},
\]
with the same \(b/c\) root-free improvement after common-root core pieces are
charged.
The high-dimensional three-shift charges in this statement are themselves
determinantal.  For finite slopes, the direction matrix has the affine form
\(G_\lambda=G_v-\lambda G_u\), so either at most \(c-b\) slopes have direction
dimension \(>b\), or the pencil is persistently low-rank and both endpoint
three-shift systems have dimension \(>b\).  A moving kernel
\(Q(\lambda)=\sum_i q_i\lambda^i\) in the persistent case satisfies the endpoint
ladder \(G_vq_0=0\), \(G_vq_i-G_uq_{i-1}=0\), and \(G_uq_D=0\), so the
persistent chain starts and ends in endpoint three-shift kernels.  For image
lines \([y]\), the
three-shift common-image map has homogeneous linear entries in \(y\), so the
bad image-line locus is cut out by \((c-b)\times(c-b)\) minors of degree
\(c-b\); unless it is persistent, it has at most
\((c-b)q^{t-1}/(q-1)\) projective lines.
The endpoint and fixed-kernel three-shift ledgers are exact frontier shifts.
For any syndrome vector \(w\) and any degree-\(<c\) core direction \(Q\),
\[
H_{t,j}(w)X^iQ=0\quad(i=0,1,2)
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
H_{t+2,c-1}(w)Q=0.
\]
Thus \(G_u=H_{t+2,c-1}(u)\), \(G_v=H_{t+2,c-1}(v)\), and
\(G_\lambda=H_{t+2,c-1}(v-\lambda u)\).  This is a lossless residual-depth
frontier shift from \((t,j)\) to \((t+2,j-2)\), not a new kind of row-rank
condition.
The same identity holds for every consecutive shift stack: for degree-\(<c\)
directions \(Q\),
\[
  H_{t,c+r-1}(w)X^iQ=0\quad(0\le i\le r)
  \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
  H_{t+r,c-1}(w)Q=0.
\]
Thus a consecutive frontier descent adds \(r\) Hankel rows and removes \(r\)
locator degrees without a per-shift algebraic loss; any lower-depth rank or
endpoint charge bound for the deeper window is consumed once by the whole
shift stack.
The finite-slope charge over such a consecutive stack has the same no-loss
form.  For
\[
  G_\lambda^{(r)}=H_{t+r,c-1}(v-\lambda u),
\]
the high-dimensional slope set
\(\{\lambda:\dim\ker G_\lambda^{(r)}>b\}\) has size at most \(c-b\), unless
all \((c-b)\times(c-b)\) minors vanish identically in \(\lambda\).  In that
persistent case both deeper endpoint maps \(H_{t+r,c-1}(u)\) and
\(H_{t+r,c-1}(v)\) have kernel dimension \(>b\), and a moving kernel satisfies
the same endpoint ladder between those deeper windows.  Thus iterating the
frontier does not introduce a multiplicative finite-slope loss.
The three-shift common-image ledger has a parallel sliding-window reduction.
For a projective image line \([y]\), let \(W_y\subseteq \F^{t+2}\) be the set
of length-\((t+2)\) vectors whose three consecutive length-\(t\) windows all
lie in \(\F y\).  Then \(\dim W_y\le1\), and \(W_y\ne0\) only for the
extended geometric shift lines
\[
  [1:\theta:\theta^2:\cdots:\theta^{t-1}]\quad(\theta\in\F),
  \qquad [0:\cdots:0:1].
\]
Moreover
\[
\ker C_y^G
=\{Q: H_{t+2,c-1}(u)Q\in W_y,\,
        H_{t+2,c-1}(v)Q\in W_y\}.
\]
Thus every common-image line off this \(q+1\)-point shift curve collapses to
the deeper endpoint intersection
\(\ker H_{t+2,c-1}(u)\cap\ker H_{t+2,c-1}(v)\); after that endpoint
intersection is charged, only the shift-persistent image lines require a
separate common-image charge.
Those shift-persistent lines are endpoint ledgers after one more exact
rewrite.  For finite \(\theta\), put
\[
  (\Delta_\theta w)_a=w_{a+1}-\theta w_a.
\]
Then
\[
\ker C_{[1:\theta:\cdots:\theta^{t-1}]}^G
=\ker H_{t+1,c-1}(\Delta_\theta u)
 \cap \ker H_{t+1,c-1}(\Delta_\theta v),
\]
while the infinity line gives
\[
\ker C_{[0:\cdots:0:1]}^G
=\ker H_{t+1,c-1}(u)\cap\ker H_{t+1,c-1}(v).
\]
Hence the residual common-image charge is no longer a projective image-line
family: it is the \(q\) first-difference endpoint intersections plus the
ordinary endpoint intersection.
This endpoint-only behavior is also stable under consecutive frontier stacks.
For \(r\ge1\), if \(W_y^{(r)}\subseteq\F^{t+r}\) consists of vectors whose
consecutive length-\(t\) windows all lie in \(\F y\), then
\(\dim W_y^{(r)}\le1\), and \(W_y^{(r)}\ne0\) only for the same \(q+1\)
extended geometric shift lines.  Off that shift curve the common-image stack
is the deeper endpoint intersection
\[
  \ker H_{t+r,c-1}(u)\cap\ker H_{t+r,c-1}(v),
\]
while a finite shift line \([1:\theta:\cdots:\theta^{t-1}]\) gives the
first-difference endpoint intersection
\[
  \ker H_{t+r-1,c-1}(\Delta_\theta u)
  \cap
  \ker H_{t+r-1,c-1}(\Delta_\theta v),
\]
and the infinity line gives the ordinary endpoint intersection at depth
\(t+r-1\).  Thus no new projective image-line family appears at deeper
consecutive frontiers.
The resulting finite first-difference parameters obey the same determinant
dichotomy.  For the stacked map
\[
J_\theta^{(r)}Q=
\bigl(H_{t+r-1,c-1}(\Delta_\theta u)Q,\,
      H_{t+r-1,c-1}(\Delta_\theta v)Q\bigr),
\]
the set where \(\dim\ker J_\theta^{(r)}>b\) has size at most \(c-b\), unless
the ordinary endpoint stack and the shifted endpoint stack both have kernel
dimension \(>b\).  In the persistent case a moving first-difference kernel
satisfies the corresponding endpoint ladder.  Thus the shift-persistent
first-difference ledgers also avoid a multiplicative per-frontier parameter
loss.
Combining these alternatives gives an additive consecutive-frontier closure.
If
\[
\dim\ker H_{t+r,c-1}(u),\ \dim\ker H_{t+r,c-1}(v),\
\dim\ker B^{(r)},\ \dim\ker A^{(r)} \le b,
\]
where \(B^{(r)}\) is the ordinary stacked endpoint map for \((u,v)\) and
\(A^{(r)}\) is the shifted stacked endpoint map for \((Su,Sv)\), then the
persistent alternatives are impossible.  After charging at most \(c-b\)
fixed-kernel slopes and at most \(c-b\) first-difference parameters, every
uncharged finite fixed-kernel and consecutive common-image frontier ledger has
direction dimension at most \(b\).  Thus this frontier step costs at most
\(2(c-b)\) finite parameter systems and no projective image-line multiplier,
independent of the number \(r\) of consecutive shifts.
As before, the endpoint hypotheses have a short-annihilator form.  Put
\(h=c-b\).  If the four short maps
\[
H_{t+r,h-1}(u),\quad H_{t+r,h-1}(v),\quad
B_h^{(r)},\quad A_h^{(r)}
\]
are injective on degree-\(<h\) directions, then the four endpoint dimensions
above are at most \(b\).  Therefore the same consecutive frontier closure
holds after charging at most \(2h\) finite parameter systems.  The row-count
thresholds for this short route are \(h\le t+r\) for the single endpoint maps
and \(h\le2(t+r-1)\) for the stacked maps.
For a finite ladder of depths \(R\), if these four short checks hold for every
\(r\in R\), the charge is additive:
\[
  |\{(r,\lambda):r\in R,\ \lambda\in E_{r,>b}\}
    \cup
    \{(r,\theta):r\in R,\ \theta\in\Theta_{r,>b}\}|
  \le 2h|R|.
\]
After those depth-indexed systems are charged, all uncharged finite
fixed-kernel and consecutive common-image frontier ledgers at depths in \(R\)
have direction dimension at most \(b\).  Thus a ladder of consecutive
frontiers pays a sum over rungs, not a product of losses.
When one of the four short frontier checks fails, the failure is itself
structured: it is a short denominator recurrence for \(u\), for \(v\), for the
ordinary pair \((u,v)\), or for the shifted pair \((Su,Sv)\).  Equivalently,
for \(Q^*(T)=D(T)\),
\[
  D(T)W_w(T)\equiv N(T)\pmod {T^{e+s}},
  \qquad \deg N<e,
\]
componentwise in the paired cases.  Domain-root factors strip losslessly by
the same \(\Delta_A\) identity as before, so short frontier failures split
into fixed-root/root-slice charges plus root-free denominator-recurrence
obstructions.
Therefore, for a finite frontier ladder, after fixed-root/root-slice short
recurrence pieces are charged, the only uncharged obstruction to applying the
additive ladder closure is a root-free recurrence in one of the four families
\[
u,\qquad v,\qquad (u,v),\qquad (Su,Sv)
\]
at one of the ladder depths.  The frontier problem is thereby reduced to a
root-free denominator-recurrence target rather than a new rank object.
In the half-window range \(h\le t+r\), these root-free residuals compress
further.  Each scalar family \(u\) or \(v\) has one primitive
reciprocal-domain-pole-free denominator, and each paired family \((u,v)\) or
\((Su,Sv)\) has one primitive vector denominator, with all other certificates
lying in the explicit multiplier ledger \(D_0M\), \(\deg M\le h-1-\deg D_0\).
Thus the frontier residual becomes a primitive Pade/residue-denominator target
for one of four objects, rather than an unstructured set of short witnesses.
There is also a nested-ladder sharpening.  If \(r_0=\min R\) and the four
short frontier checks hold at \(r_0\), then they hold at every deeper
\(r\in R\), and
\[
  E_{r,>b}\subseteq E_{r_0,>b},\qquad
  \Theta_{r,>b}\subseteq\Theta_{r_0,>b}.
\]
Thus charging the bottom-rung sets \(E_{r_0,>b}\) and
\(\Theta_{r_0,>b}\), of total size at most \(2h\), closes the finite
fixed-kernel and consecutive common-image ledgers at every depth in \(R\).
For nested frontier ladders, the finite parameter charge is paid at the bottom
rung.
The root-free residuals nest in the same way.  Any root-free recurrence witness
for \(u\), \(v\), \((u,v)\), or \((Su,Sv)\) at a deeper \(r\in R\) is already
a witness for the same family at \(r_0\).  Hence, after fixed-root/root-slice
pieces are charged, the bottom root-free recurrence families control the whole
ladder; in the half-window range \(h\le t+r_0\), the bottom primitive
denominator targets control all deeper residuals.
Consequently a finite nested frontier ladder closes from bottom-rung data:
charge the fixed-root/root-slice short recurrence pieces, charge the four
bottom root-free recurrence families, and charge the bottom finite parameter
sets \(E_{r_0,>b}\cup\Theta_{r_0,>b}\), of total size at most \(2h\).  In the
half-window range the root-free recurrence charges may be recorded as primitive
denominator targets.  No further \(|R|\) factor, projective image-line factor,
or per-rung multiplier is introduced by the consecutive frontier descent.
The two paired bottom residuals also have a controlled overlap.  A witness
lying in both the ordinary pair \((u,v)\) and shifted pair \((Su,Sv)\)
recurrences is exactly a witness for the deeper endpoint-pair recurrence
\[
  H_{t+r,h-1}(u)Q=H_{t+r,h-1}(v)Q=0.
\]
Thus, after the endpoint-pair residual is charged, the ordinary and shifted
paired targets share no root-free witness; in the half-window language, a
primitive denominator common to both paired targets is an endpoint-pair charge.
More generally, an \((L+1)\)-block of consecutive shifted paired residuals at
window \(t+r-1\) intersects exactly in the ordinary paired residual at depth
\(r+L\).  Thus consecutive paired-residual overlap is paid at the deepest pair,
not once per adjacent shift.
If a finite ladder begins before the half-window range, there is also a sharp
cutoff: once \(h\le t+r\) first holds, every later root-free residual is already
contained in one of the four residual families at that first half-window rung.
The primitive-denominator charge for the half-window tail is therefore paid
once at the cutoff; only the earlier pre-half-window rungs remain longer-Pade
targets.
This gives a mixed-ladder closure ledger: after fixed-root/root-slice charges,
charge the pre-half-window longer-Pade residual families, charge the four
primitive denominator targets at the first half-window rung when it exists, and
then charge only the bottom finite parameter systems, of total size at most
\(2h\) on the remaining ledger.  The consecutive common-image part introduces
neither a half-window-depth multiplier nor a projective image-line multiplier.
The cutoff control is also denominator-theoretic: for any one of the four
residual families, every deeper half-window primitive denominator is divisible
by the primitive denominator at the first half-window rung.  Thus deeper tail
denominators are refinements inside the cutoff multiplier ledger, not new
primitive bases.  Over \(\F_q\), if the cutoff primitive denominator has
degree \(\delta\), the whole half-window tail for that family has at most
\((q^{h-\delta}-1)/(q-1)\) ambient projective certificate-denominator
classes, and hence at most that many primitive denominator classes, before the
deeper truncation equations are imposed.  This is independent of the number of
half-window depths.  Summed over the four residual families, the
family-labelled tail budget is
\[
  \sum_F {q^{h-\delta_F}-1\over q-1}
  \le 4{q^h-1\over q-1},
\]
where absent cutoff families contribute zero.  This is a depth-independent
denominator-class budget for the half-window tail.  The ordinary and shifted
paired tails also have controlled overlap: any projective
certificate-denominator class appearing in both is already an endpoint-pair
certificate at the first half-window rung, so after the endpoint-pair residual
is charged their intersection contributes no new tail budget.  Scalar-paired
tail intersections are likewise explicit: they are one-sided cutoff endpoint
residuals, with one endpoint in the longer \(t+r\) window and the other in the
paired \(t+r-1\) window.  After these one-sided residuals are charged,
scalar-paired intersections also contribute no new unlabelled tail
certificate-denominator classes.  Finally, the two scalar tails
intersect only through the same endpoint-pair residual.  Hence the four-family
tail budget now has an explicit pairwise-overlap classification: scalar-scalar
and paired-paired overlaps are endpoint-pair charges, while scalar-paired
overlaps are one-sided endpoint charges.  Once those named overlap residuals
and their multiplier ledgers are charged, the remaining half-window tail is
family-disjoint: every uncharged projective certificate-denominator class, and
hence every primitive denominator class, has a unique residual-family label, so
the depth-independent family sum is an ordinary disjoint denominator ledger.
The named cutoff overlap charges are themselves half-window Pade ledgers.  At
the cutoff depth \(r_{\rm hw}\), the endpoint-pair residual uses the paired
parent \((u,v)\) at window \(t+r_{\rm hw}\), and the four one-sided residuals
use either the paired parent \((u,v)\) or the shifted paired parent \((Su,Sv)\)
at window \(t+r_{\rm hw}-1\).  Thus each active overlap system has one parent
primitive vector denominator and an explicit projective multiplier ledger of
size at most
\((q^{h-\delta}-1)/(q-1)\); the four one-sided systems may impose one extra
linear row on that multiplier ledger.  Crude summation gives at most
\(5(q^h-1)/(q-1)\) ambient projective certificate-denominator classes for all
cutoff overlap charges before those extra rows are imposed.
This overlap budget is not independent of the family budget.  Each overlap
denominator is divisible by the least common multiple of the primitive
denominators for the cutoff families it intersects: the endpoint-pair overlap
uses \(\operatorname{lcm}(D_u,D_v,D_{uv},D_S)\), while the four one-sided
overlaps use \(\operatorname{lcm}(D_u,D_{uv})\),
\(\operatorname{lcm}(D_v,D_{uv})\), \(\operatorname{lcm}(D_u,D_S)\), and
\(\operatorname{lcm}(D_v,D_S)\).  Thus the overlap ledgers are intersection
ledgers of the existing family primitives, and their multiplier counts improve
with the lcm degree.
There is also a locator-space row-cut dichotomy for the four one-sided
overlaps.  Inside the ordinary paired parent kernel
\(\ker H_s(u)\cap\ker H_s(v)\), the systems \((u\mid u,v)\) and
\((v\mid u,v)\) add respectively the missing rows \(\operatorname{row}_s(u)\)
and \(\operatorname{row}_s(v)\).  Inside the shifted paired parent
\(\ker H_s(Su)\cap\ker H_s(Sv)\), the systems \((u\mid Su,Sv)\) and
\((v\mid Su,Sv)\) add the missing rows \(\operatorname{row}_0(u)\) and
\(\operatorname{row}_0(v)\).  If the added row is nonzero on the parent
kernel, the one-sided overlap loses one projective dimension; otherwise the
parent paired kernel is already endpoint-persistent in that direction.
This persistence alternative is itself a concrete stacked-Hankel row-span
condition: the missing row must lie in the row span of the paired parent
matrix.  The endpoint-pair overlap has the two-row version: its codimension
inside \(\ker H_s(u)\cap\ker H_s(v)\) is the rank of the two restricted
endpoint rows \(\operatorname{row}_s(u)\) and \(\operatorname{row}_s(v)\).
Hence the full endpoint-pair ledger loses two dimensions unless those two rows
are dependent modulo the ordinary paired parent row span.
Writing \(d_{uv}=\dim(\ker H_s(u)\cap\ker H_s(v))\), \(d_S=\dim(\ker H_s(Su)\cap
\ker H_s(Sv))\), and letting \(r_{EP}\) and the four \(\epsilon\)'s denote
these restricted endpoint-row ranks, the whole cutoff-overlap projective
locator budget is bounded by
\[
  \Phi(d_{uv}-r_{EP})+\Phi(d_{uv}-\epsilon_u)+\Phi(d_{uv}-\epsilon_v)
  +\Phi(d_S-\epsilon_{uS})+\Phi(d_S-\epsilon_{vS}),
  \qquad \Phi(m)={q^m-1\over q-1}.
\]
Root-free certificate-denominator classes are bounded by the same expression.
Combining this with the lcm multiplier ledgers gives a termwise hybrid
overlap budget: for each overlap system one may take the minimum of the
family-lcm multiplier count and the endpoint-row rank count.  For example, the
endpoint-pair summand is
\[
  \min\!\left\{\Phi(h-\deg \operatorname{lcm}(D_u,D_v,D_{uv},D_S)),
               \Phi(d_{uv}-r_{EP})\right\},
\]
and the four one-sided summands have the analogous lcm/rank minima.  Thus a
large lcm degree and a genuine endpoint-row cut are independent ways to shrink
the same cutoff-overlap charge.
On the rank side, the endpoint-pair residual is not a fifth independent
summand: it is contained in each one-sided overlap space.  Thus the projective
rank budget for the union of cutoff overlap systems can be sharpened by
inclusion-exclusion to
\[
  \Phi(d_{uv}-\epsilon_u)+\Phi(d_{uv}-\epsilon_v)-\Phi(d_{uv}-r_{EP})
 +\Phi(d_S-\epsilon_{uS})+\Phi(d_S-\epsilon_{vS})-\Phi(d_S-r_S),
\]
where \(r_S\) is the rank of the two missing lower endpoint rows on the shifted
paired parent kernel.
In refined mixed-ladder form, after the pre-half-window residuals and the raw
half-window tail divisor-arrangement ledger are charged, the only remaining
finite frontier charge is the bottom finite parameter set
\(E_{r_0,>b}\cup\Theta_{r_0,>b}\), of size at most \(2h\) on the remaining
ledger.  Equivalently, apart from the pre-half-window longer-Pade residual
families, the current direct raw-denominator upper ledger is
\[
  \mathrm{ArrBudget}_{hw}+2h.
\]
The tail term is the divisor arrangement of the active cutoff primitive
denominators:
\[
  \mathrm{ArrBudget}_{hw}
  =\sum_{\emptyset\ne I\subseteq A}(-1)^{|I|+1}
     \Phi_+\!\left(h-\deg \operatorname{lcm}_{F\in I}D_F\right),
  \qquad
  \Phi_+(m)=\Phi(\max\{m,0\}),
\]
and
\[
  |\mathrm{TailUnion}_{hw}|
  \le \mathrm{ArrBudget}_{hw}
  \le \mathrm{FamilyBudget}_{hw}.
\]
The same arrangement may be computed after deleting every active family whose
cutoff primitive denominator is a multiple of another active family's
primitive denominator and after identifying duplicate primitive denominators.
Thus only the distinct divisibility-minimal antichain of active primitive
denominators contributes to the raw tail union; if the active denominators
form a chain, then
\[
  \mathrm{ArrBudget}_{hw}=\Phi(h-\delta_{\min})
\]
for the smallest active primitive denominator \(D_{\min}\), with the convention
that the right-hand side is zero when \(\delta_{\min}\ge h\).
More generally, if the surviving antichain has common core
\[
  C=\gcd(D:D\in M_{\min}), \qquad \gamma=\deg C,
\]
then multiplication by \(C\) identifies the raw tail arrangement with the
quotient arrangement for the denominators \(D/C\) in the shorter window
\(h-\gamma\).  In particular,
\[
  \mathrm{ArrBudget}_{hw}\le \Phi_+(h-\gamma),
  \qquad
  \mathrm{ArrBudget}_{hw}+2h\le \Phi_+(h-\gamma)+2h,
\]
so the half-window tail vanishes from the raw denominator ledger when
\(\gamma\ge h\).  If those quotient denominators are pairwise coprime, the exact
tail term is
\[
  \mathrm{ArrBudget}_{hw}
  =\sum_{\emptyset\ne I\subseteq M_{\min}}(-1)^{|I|+1}
     \Phi_+\!\left(h-\gamma-\sum_{D\in I}\deg(D/C)\right).
\]
Without coprimality, if \(s=|M_{\min}|\) and
\(e_{\min}=\min_{D\in M_{\min}}\deg(D/C)\), then
\[
  \mathrm{ArrBudget}_{hw}\le s\,\Phi_+(h-\gamma-e_{\min}).
\]
When \(s\ge2\), one has \(e_{\min}\ge1\), so every genuinely multi-family
antichain gains at least one quotient degree beyond the common-core ambient
bound.  Hence if \(\gamma+e_{\min}\ge h-L\), then
\[
  \mathrm{ArrBudget}_{hw}+2h \le 4\Phi_+(L)+2h.
\]
For \(q\le n^a\), \(h\le n\), and fixed \(L\ge1\), this is at most
\[
  4L n^{a(L-1)}+2n.
\]
There is also a bottom residual route: charging the four root-free residual
families at \(r_0=\min R\) removes every residual at every depth in the ladder,
so the pre-half part has no \(|R_{\mathrm{pre}}|\) factor.  This route closes
with only the same bottom finite frontier charge \(2h\), while the half-window
arrangement route is useful when the bottom longer-Pade residuals are left as
the named obstruction.
Equivalently, the residual bottleneck can be recorded as a two-route minimum.
If \(B_0\) is the union of the four bottom root-free residual families and
\(P_{\mathrm{pre}}\) is the union of the four pre-half residual families over
all \(r\in R_{\mathrm{pre}}\), then any admissible charges for these sets give
the consumable ledger
\[
  \min\{\mathrm{Charge}(B_0),
          \mathrm{Charge}(P_{\mathrm{pre}})
          +\mathrm{ArrBudget}_{hw}\}+2h.
\]
This does not bound \(B_0\) or \(P_{\mathrm{pre}}\) by itself; it makes the
current M1 bottleneck explicit: either prove the bottom longer-Pade family is
small enough, or leave the pre-half family as the named obstruction and prove
the half-window arrangement term is small.
In the common two-denominator case \(M_{\min}=\{D_1,D_2\}\), writing
\(C=\gcd(D_1,D_2)\), \(D_i=C E_i\), and \(e_i=\deg E_i\), the quotient
denominators \(E_1,E_2\) are automatically coprime and
\[
  \mathrm{ArrBudget}_{hw}
  =\Phi_+(h-\gamma-e_1)+\Phi_+(h-\gamma-e_2)
   -\Phi_+(h-\gamma-e_1-e_2).
\]
The named overlap ledgers remain useful diagnostics:
\[
  \mathrm{OverlapSep}_{hw}
  =\min\{\mathrm{SideHybridOverlap}_{hw},\mathrm{UnionRankOverlap}_{hw}\},
\]
where \(\mathrm{SideHybridOverlap}_{hw}\) is the four one-sided hybrid overlap
sum and the endpoint-pair residual is omitted as a separate hybrid summand
because it is contained in the one-sided overlap union.  These overlap ledgers
are not additional denominator-class charges in the direct raw-tail closure,
since the overlap systems are already subsets of
\(\mathrm{TailUnion}_{hw}\).
Equivalently, the full three-shift common-image branch is endpoint-only.  If
\(\mathrm{GCI}\) denotes the monic split core locators satisfying some
three-shift common-image line, then
\[
\mathrm{GCI}
=E_{\mathrm{deep}}\cup E_\infty\cup\bigcup_{\theta\in\F}E_\theta,
\]
where \(E_{\mathrm{deep}}\) is cut out by
\(H_{t+2,c}(u)L=H_{t+2,c}(v)L=0\), \(E_\infty\) by
\(H_{t+1,c}(u)L=H_{t+1,c}(v)L=0\), and \(E_\theta\) by the two
first-difference equations above.  Thus, once these endpoint-type ledgers have
direction dimension at most \(b\), this whole common-image branch contributes
at most \((q+2)\binom{n}{b}\), with the same root-free \(b/c\) replacement
after common-root core pieces are charged.
Combining this with the globally full core classification gives an endpointized
closure: globally full cores are covered by
\[
U,\ V,\ D_\infty,\ \{K_\lambda\}_{\lambda\in\F},\
\{D_\theta\}_{\theta\in\F},
\]
where \(U,V\) are the two deeper endpoint systems,
\(K_\lambda\) is the deeper fixed-kernel endpoint system for
\(v-\lambda u\), \(D_\infty\) is the ordinary endpoint intersection, and
\(D_\theta\) is the first-difference endpoint intersection.  If all these
endpoint-type charge systems have direction dimension at most \(b\), then
\[
|\mathrm{GlobalFullCore}|\le (2q+3)\binom{n}{b},
\]
again with the root-free \(b/c\) replacement after common-root pieces are
charged.
Combining this endpointized global bound with the non-global anchor-multiplicity
collapse gives the corresponding all-anchor full-core closure:
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal Z_{\rm all}^{\rm endpoint,\le b}|
&\le
\left(\frac{2q}{m}+2(n-m+1)\right)\binom{n}{m-1}\\
&\quad + q(n-m+1)(2q+3)\binom{n}{b}.
\end{aligned}
\]
After common-root global core pieces are charged, the final term gains the same
factor \(b/c\).  Thus the globally full branch of the all-anchor full-core
component no longer carries the projective common-image factor
\((q^t-1)/(q-1)\); the remaining large-rank work is concentrated in the
endpointized charge systems and the separate nondegenerate shadow ledger.
The first-difference endpoint systems themselves are determinantal.  With
\(\Delta_\theta w=w^+-\theta w\), the stacked direction matrix
\[
J_\theta Q=
\bigl(H_{t+1,c-1}(\Delta_\theta u)Q,\,
      H_{t+1,c-1}(\Delta_\theta v)Q\bigr)
\]
has affine form \(J_\theta=J_+-\theta J_0\).  Hence either at most \(c-b\)
parameters \(\theta\) have direction dimension \(>b\), or the pencil is
persistently low-rank.  In the persistent case both endpoint intersections
\(J_0\) and \(J_+\) have direction dimension \(>b\), and any moving kernel
\(Q(\theta)=\sum_iq_i\theta^i\) satisfies
\[
J_+q_0=0,\qquad J_+q_i-J_0q_{i-1}=0,\qquad J_0q_D=0.
\]
Thus the remaining high-dimensional first-difference charges are finite
exceptions or explicit moving-kernel certificates between endpoint
intersections.
The endpointized finite-exception charge therefore reduces to four base
endpoint checks.  If
\[
\dim\ker H_{t+2,c-1}(u)\le b,\quad
\dim\ker H_{t+2,c-1}(v)\le b,\quad
\dim\ker J_0\le b,\quad
\dim\ker J_+ \le b,
\]
then neither the fixed-kernel pencil nor the first-difference pencil can be
persistent.  Hence the high-dimensional finite-parameter sets satisfy
\[
|\Lambda_{K,>b}|\le c-b,\qquad |\Theta_{D,>b}|\le c-b.
\]
After charging these at most \(2(c-b)\) endpointized exceptional systems,
the preceding global full-core and all-anchor full-core bounds apply to the
remaining ledgers.
The base checks and exceptional parameters have a short-annihilator
certificate form.  Put \(h=c-b\).  Any endpointized matrix on degree-\(<c\)
directions with kernel dimension \(>b\) has a nonzero kernel vector supported
in degrees \(<h\).  Consequently
\[
\Lambda_{K,>b}\subseteq
\{\lambda:\ker H_{t+2,h-1}(v-\lambda u)\ne0\},
\]
and
\[
\Theta_{D,>b}\subseteq
\{\theta:\ker J_\theta^{(h)}\ne0\},
\quad
J_\theta^{(h)}Q=
\bigl(H_{t+1,h-1}(\Delta_\theta u)Q,\,
      H_{t+1,h-1}(\Delta_\theta v)Q\bigr).
\]
Likewise, failure of one of the four base endpoint checks forces a nonzero
kernel in one of
\[
H_{t+2,h-1}(u),\quad H_{t+2,h-1}(v),\quad
J_0^{(h)},\quad J_+^{(h)}.
\]
Thus the next endpoint obstruction is a short-denominator recurrence
certificate, not an unstructured large-kernel event.
The corresponding bad parameters are themselves projective landing images.
For \(Q\ne0\) of degree \(<h\), put
\[
U_h(Q)=H_{t+2,h-1}(u)Q,\qquad V_h(Q)=H_{t+2,h-1}(v)Q.
\]
If there is no common short kernel \(U_h(Q)=V_h(Q)=0\), then every short
fixed-kernel parameter is the unique scalar attached to a projective locator
\([Q]\) with \(U_h(Q)\ne0\) and
\[
\operatorname{rank}[\,U_h(Q)\ V_h(Q)\,]\le1.
\]
The first-difference parameters have the same form with the stacked endpoint
vectors
\[
P_0^h(Q)=
\bigl(H_{t+1,h-1}(u)Q,\ H_{t+1,h-1}(v)Q\bigr),\quad
P_+^h(Q)=
\bigl(H_{t+1,h-1}(u^+)Q,\ H_{t+1,h-1}(v^+)Q\bigr).
\]
Unless \(P_0^h\) and \(P_+^h\) have a common short kernel, each bad
\(\theta\) is the unique scalar for a projective \([Q]\) satisfying
\(\operatorname{rank}[\,P_0^h(Q)\ P_+^h(Q)\,]\le1\).  Thus the endpoint
exceptional sets reduce to projective short-locator landing varieties plus
common short endpoint kernels.
There is also a one-sided injection test.  If either
\[
H_{t+2,h-1}(u)\quad\text{or}\quad H_{t+2,h-1}(v)
\]
is injective on degree-\(<h\) directions, then a nonzero \(h\times h\)
minor of \(H_{t+2,h-1}(v-\lambda u)\) cuts all short fixed-kernel parameters,
so \(|\Lambda_h|\le h\), and hence \(|\Lambda_{K,>b}|\le h\).  Likewise, if
either \(J_0^{(h)}\) or \(J_+^{(h)}\) is injective, then
\(|\Theta_h|\le h\), and hence \(|\Theta_{D,>b}|\le h\).  Any larger short
exception family must therefore already expose short endpoint annihilators on
both sides of the relevant pencil.
Finally, short annihilators split cleanly into fixed-root and root-free parts.
For \(\alpha\in\F\), \((\Delta_\alpha w)_a=w_{a+1}-\alpha w_a\), and if
\(Q=(X-\alpha)R\), then
\[
H_{s,d}(w)Q=H_{s,d-1}(\Delta_\alpha w)R.
\]
Iterating, \(Q=L_A R\) gives
\[
H_{s,d}(w)Q=H_{s,d-|A|}(\Delta_A w)R.
\]
Thus a short fixed-kernel witness with a domain-root factor \(L_A\) becomes a
short witness for the differenced pencil
\[
H_{t+2,h-|A|-1}(\Delta_Av-\lambda\Delta_Au)R=0,
\]
and a first-difference witness becomes the pair of equations for
\(\Delta_A\Delta_\theta u\) and \(\Delta_A\Delta_\theta v\).  The remaining
endpoint obstruction is therefore the root-free short-annihilator family after
fixed-root/root-slice factors have been charged.
The first-difference short parameters can also be viewed as marked roots of
ordinary common endpoint recurrences.  Let
\[
C_h^+=\{P:\deg P\le h,\ H_{t+1,h}(u)P=H_{t+1,h}(v)P=0\}.
\]
Then
\[
\ker J_\theta^{(h)}
\cong \{P\in C_h^+:P(\theta)=0\},
\qquad Q\mapsto (X-\theta)Q.
\]
Hence
\[
\Theta_h=\{\theta:\exists\,0\ne P\in C_h^+,\ P(\theta)=0\}.
\]
Over \(\F_q\), if \(g=\dim C_h^+\), this gives
\[
|\Theta_h|\le \min\left(q,\ h\,\frac{q^g-1}{q-1}\right).
\]
In particular, a one-dimensional common recurrence space contributes at most
\(h\) first-difference parameters.
The short fixed-kernel parameters have the parallel finite/persistent
dichotomy.  For
\[
U_hQ=H_{t+2,h-1}(u)Q,\qquad V_hQ=H_{t+2,h-1}(v)Q,
\]
either some \(h\times h\) minor of \(V_h-\lambda U_h\) is nonzero, in which
case \(|\Lambda_h|\le h\) and hence \(|\Lambda_{K,>b}|\le h\), or every such
minor vanishes identically.  In the persistent case there is a nonzero moving
short kernel \(Q(\lambda)=\sum_i q_i\lambda^i\), and its coefficients satisfy
\[
V_hq_0=0,\qquad V_hq_i-U_hq_{i-1}=0,\qquad U_hq_D=0.
\]
Thus the remaining fixed-kernel obstruction is finite unless it is an explicit
moving short-recurrence certificate between the two short endpoint kernels.
Common root factors in these moving certificates also strip losslessly.  If
\[
Q(\lambda,X)=L_A(X)R(\lambda,X)
\]
is a moving fixed-kernel certificate, then
\[
H_{t+2,h-|A|-1}(\Delta_Av-\lambda\Delta_Au)R(\lambda,X)=0.
\]
Likewise a moving first-difference certificate with the same common factor
strips to the two equations for
\(\Delta_A\Delta_\theta u\) and \(\Delta_A\Delta_\theta v\).  Therefore the
moving-certificate alternatives can be split into fixed-root/root-slice pieces
and common-root-free moving short certificates.
These reductions give a concise closure criterion.  If the four short endpoint
systems
\[
H_{t+2,h-1}(u),\quad H_{t+2,h-1}(v),\quad J_0^{(h)},\quad J_+^{(h)}
\]
are injective, then the four base endpoint checks hold, and
\[
|\Lambda_{K,>b}|\le h,\qquad |\Theta_{D,>b}|\le h.
\]
After charging these at most \(2h\) endpointized exceptional systems, every
remaining endpointized system in the global full-core ledger has direction
dimension at most \(b\), so the endpointized global full-core and all-anchor
full-core bounds apply.
The four short injectivity checks are explicit Hankel-minor targets.  A single
short map \(H_{s,h-1}(w):\F^h\to\F^s\) can be injective only if \(h\le s\),
and for \(h\le s\) the injectivity locus is a nonempty determinantal open
condition.  A stacked pair
\[
Q\mapsto (H_{s,h-1}(a)Q,\ H_{s,h-1}(b)Q)
\]
can be injective only if \(h\le2s\), and for \(h\le2s\) its injectivity locus
is also nonempty determinantal open.  Thus Corollary 53 is dimensionally
available only when \(h\le t+2\) for the two single endpoint checks and
\(h\le2(t+1)\) for \(J_0^{(h)}\) and \(J_+^{(h)}\); outside these ranges the
short-annihilator obstruction is row-count forced.
Equivalently, since \(h=c-b\), this route has the threshold
\[
b\ge b_{\min}:=\max(0,c-(t+2)).
\]
For \(b<b_{\min}\), the single endpoint maps cannot be injective by row count.
For \(b\ge b_{\min}\), all four short checks are dimensionally feasible and
the endpointized charge is reduced to explicit nonvanishing of four
\(h\times h\) Hankel minors plus the finite \(2h\)-exception charge.
Finally, a short Hankel kernel is exactly a denominator recurrence.  If
\(Q(X)=\sum_iq_iX^i\) has degree \(<h\), then
\[
H_{s,h-1}(w)Q=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_i q_i w_{a+i}=0\quad(0\le a<s).
\]
After the root-stripping reductions, the remaining short failures are
\(D\)-root-free denominator recurrences: for fixed-kernel parameters, a
root-free \(Q\) annihilates the combined syndrome \(v-\lambda u\); for
first-difference parameters, the same root-free \(Q\) annihilates both
\(\Delta_\theta u\) and \(\Delta_\theta v\).  Thus the endpoint-short
obstruction is now expressed in the same denominator language as the
residue-line packing problem.
Equivalently, if \(Q(X)=q_0+\cdots+q_eX^e\),
\[
Q^*(T)=q_e+q_{e-1}T+\cdots+q_0T^e,\qquad
W_w(T)=\sum_{a\ge0}w_aT^a,
\]
then
\[
H_{s,h-1}(w)Q=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Q^*(T)W_w(T)\equiv N_{Q,w}(T)\pmod {T^{e+s}},
\qquad \deg N_{Q,w}<e.
\]
Thus the remaining root-free fixed-kernel and first-difference failures are
truncated Pade/residue-denominator certificates for \(v-\lambda u\) and for
the pair \((\Delta_\theta u,\Delta_\theta v)\), respectively.
Moreover, the root-free condition has the expected reciprocal-pole meaning:
for \(\alpha\ne0\),
\[
Q(\alpha)=\alpha^eQ^*(1/\alpha).
\]
Thus \(D\)-root-free \(Q\) is equivalent to \(Q^*\) having no zeros on the
reciprocal domain \(\{1/\alpha:\alpha\in D,\alpha\ne0\}\), together with no
zero at the projective point \(\infty\) when \(0\in D\).  Fixed-root/root-slice
charges are therefore exactly the reciprocal-domain denominator factors
removed before the root-free residue-denominator obstruction remains.
There is also a half-window uniqueness compression.  If
\[
D_i(T)W_w(T)\equiv N_i(T)\pmod {T^{e_i+s}},\qquad \deg N_i<e_i
\quad (i=1,2),
\]
and \(s\ge\max(e_1,e_2)\), then
\[
N_1D_2=N_2D_1.
\]
Indeed the difference is divisible by
\(T^{\min(e_1+s,e_2+s)}\) but has degree \(<e_1+e_2\).  Thus all
order-\(\le s\) denominator certificates for one syndrome series have the
same reduced rational function; componentwise, common certificates for a pair
of series have one primitive vector denominator dividing every common
certificate denominator.  In this range the root-free endpoint-short
obstruction is reduced to primitive reciprocal-domain-pole-free denominators
and root-free multipliers; outside it, the remaining obstruction is a genuine
longer vector-Pade problem.
If the primitive denominator has degree \(\delta\), then every order-\(<h\)
certificate denominator lies in
\[
D(T)=D_0(T)M(T),\qquad \deg M\le h-1-\delta.
\]
Thus, over \(\F_q\), the projective multiplier ledger attached to a fixed
primitive denominator has size at most
\[
1+q+\cdots+q^{h-1-\delta}=\frac{q^{h-\delta}-1}{q-1},
\]
and the root-free and truncation conditions only cut this ledger down.  Hence
the half-window denominator problem is reduced to bounding primitive
reciprocal-domain-pole-free denominators, then paying this explicit multiplier
factor.
The primitive denominator itself still witnesses the obstruction: if
\(D=D_0M\) is a certificate and \(D_0\) is the reduced primitive denominator,
then \(M(0)\ne0\), so \(D_0W-N_0\) is divisible by the same initial power of
\(T\) after cancelling \(M\).  Thus multipliers affect certificate counting,
not existence.  Parameter collisions also collapse to base endpoint charges.
If the same denominator works for two distinct fixed-kernel parameters
\(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\), then subtracting
\[
W_{v-\lambda_1u}-W_{v-\lambda_2u}=(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)W_u
\]
shows that the denominator works for both \(u\) and \(v\) at depth \(t+2\).
Similarly, if the same common denominator works for two distinct
first-difference parameters \(\theta_1,\theta_2\), then
\[
W_{\Delta_{\theta_1}w}-W_{\Delta_{\theta_2}w}
      =(\theta_2-\theta_1)W_w
\]
for \(w=u,v\), so the denominator lies in the base intersection
\(\ker H_{t+1,h-1}(u)\cap\ker H_{t+1,h-1}(v)\).  After charging these base
endpoint intersections, bad parameters inject into primitive denominator
classes.
The remaining primitive denominators have an explicit rank-one landing form.
For an order-\(d\) reversed denominator \(D\), write
\[
R_s(D;w)=([T^d]DW_w,\ldots,[T^{d+s-1}]DW_w).
\]
For fixed-kernel parameters, with
\[
U_D=R_{t+2}(D;u),\qquad V_D=R_{t+2}(D;v),
\]
one has \(D\) supporting \(\lambda\) exactly when \(V_D=\lambda U_D\).
Outside the base endpoint intersection \(U_D=V_D=0\), this is the unique
rank-one landing \(V_D\in \F U_D\).  For first differences, set
\[
B_D=(R_{t+1}(D;u),R_{t+1}(D;v)),\quad
A_D=(R_{t+1}(D;Su),R_{t+1}(D;Sv)).
\]
Then \(D\) supports \(\theta\) exactly when \(A_D=\theta B_D\), and outside
\(B_D=0\) this is the unique landing \(A_D\in\F B_D\).  The entries are linear
in the coefficients of \(D\), so after endpoint charges the remaining primitive
denominator families are explicit \(2\times2\)-minor incidence loci, plus the
root-free open condition.
This covers the whole endpoint-short closure range.  At the threshold
\[
b\ge b_{\min}=\max(0,c-(t+2)),\qquad h=c-b,
\]
one has \(h\le t+2\).  Hence every order-\(<h\) fixed-kernel certificate has
order \(e\le t+2\), and every order-\(<h\) first-difference certificate has
order \(e\le h-1\le t+1\).  Thus all short certificates at the row-count
feasible threshold are in the half-window regime above; the longer vector-Pade
range only appears below the threshold where the short-injectivity route is
already impossible by row count.
Finally, each fixed denominator order has a finite-landing alternative.  In
the projective coefficient space \(P_d\), the rank-one landing equations are
homogeneous quadrics.  Therefore, if the remaining base-free root-free
order-\(d\) landing layer is zero-dimensional, projective Bezout bounds it by
\(2^d\) points.  After endpoint collision charges, bad parameters inject into
primitive denominator classes, so finite order layers contribute at most
\[
\sum_{d=0}^{h-1}2^d=2^h-1
\]
per fixed-kernel or first-difference family.  Thus the unresolved primitive
denominator obstruction is now either this explicit finite ledger or a
positive-dimensional rank-one landing component.
Such a positive-dimensional component is itself certificate-form.  Over the
function field \(K\) of an irreducible component, the generic denominator
\[
D_C(T)=a_0+\cdots+a_dT^d,\qquad a_0\ne0,
\]
is root-free on the reciprocal domain and has a unique parameter: in the
fixed-kernel case \(V_{D_C}=\lambda_CU_{D_C}\), giving
\[
D_CW_{v-\lambda_Cu}\equiv N_C\pmod {T^{d+t+2}},\qquad \deg N_C<d,
\]
and in the first-difference case \(A_{D_C}=\theta_CB_{D_C}\), giving the two
analogous congruences for \(\Delta_{\theta_C}u\) and \(\Delta_{\theta_C}v\)
modulo \(T^{d+t+1}\).  The parameter is either constant, hence a fixed-slice
charge, or genuinely moving.  Thus positive-dimensional primitive landing
components are not opaque: they are moving denominator certificates to
classify or charge.
The constant-parameter subcase is a fixed-slice recurrence charge.  For fixed
\(\lambda\),
\[
K_{d,\lambda}=\{D:R_{t+2}(D;v-\lambda u)=0\}
\]
is a linear recurrence space, and a positive-dimensional constant-\(\lambda\)
component forces \(\dim K_{d,\lambda}\ge2\).  For fixed \(\theta\),
\[
D_{d,\theta}=\{D:R_{t+1}(D;\Delta_\theta u)=
R_{t+1}(D;\Delta_\theta v)=0\}
\]
is the analogous first-difference recurrence space, and a positive-dimensional
constant-\(\theta\) component forces \(\dim D_{d,\theta}\ge2\).  Thus, after
charging high-dimensional fixed-slice recurrence spaces, any remaining
positive-dimensional primitive landing component has genuinely nonconstant
parameter.
Such a nonconstant component descends to a persistent one-parameter kernel.
With an indeterminate \(Z\), define
\[
M_d^K(Z)D=R_{t+2}(D;v-Zu),\qquad
M_d^D(Z)D=(R_{t+1}(D;\Delta_Zu),R_{t+1}(D;\Delta_Zv)).
\]
If a fixed-kernel component has nonconstant parameter, then
\(\ker M_d^K(Z)\ne0\) over \(F(Z)\); if a first-difference component has
nonconstant parameter, then \(\ker M_d^D(Z)\ne0\) over \(F(Z)\).  Conversely,
a nonzero generic kernel vector whose root-free, primitive, order-\(d\), and
base-free opens are not empty gives a positive-dimensional landing family.
Thus after constant fixed-slice charges, the moving branch is exactly a
persistent one-parameter short-recurrence kernel, not an arbitrary
positive-dimensional incidence.
In the row-count feasible range this persistent kernel already forces endpoint
rank failure.  For the fixed-kernel pencil \(M_d^K(Z)=V_d-ZU_d\), vanishing of
all full-column minors over \(F(Z)\) implies that the constant and top-degree
coefficients vanish, hence both \(V_d\) and \(U_d\) have nontrivial kernel.
For the first-difference pencil \(M_d^D(Z)=A_d-ZB_d\), the same argument forces
\(\ker A_d\ne0\) and \(\ker B_d\ne0\).  At the endpoint-short threshold
\(d<h\le t+2\), the row-count hypotheses hold for both pencils.  Therefore the
four short endpoint injectivity checks
\[
H_{t+2,h-1}(u),\quad H_{t+2,h-1}(v),\quad J_0^{(h)},\quad J_+^{(h)}
\]
rule out the persistent moving branch entirely.
The constant-parameter positive slices also have a finite/persistent
dichotomy.  For order \(1\le d<h\), the condition
\(\dim K_{d,\lambda}\ge2\) is equivalent to
\(\operatorname{rank}(V_d-\lambda U_d)\le d-1\), hence is cut out by
\(d\times d\) minors of degree at most \(d\) in \(\lambda\).  Thus either
there are at most \(d\) such \(\lambda\), or the pencil is persistently
rank-deficient and both endpoint maps \(U_d,V_d\) have two-dimensional kernel.
The same statement holds for the stacked first-difference pencil
\(A_d-\theta B_d\).  After charging endpoint maps with two-dimensional short
kernels, constant-parameter positive slices contribute at most
\[
\sum_{d=1}^{h-1}d=\frac{h(h-1)}2
\]
parameters in each family.
Combining these last alternatives gives a finite endpoint-short denominator
ledger under the same four short injectivity checks.  Nonconstant
positive-dimensional primitive landing components are impossible, because they
would be persistent one-parameter kernels and hence would force endpoint rank
failure.  Constant-parameter positive slices contribute at most
\(h(h-1)/2\) parameters per family, while the zero-dimensional primitive
landing layers contribute at most \(2^h-1\).  Thus the
denominator-geometric accounting left after fixed-root, base endpoint, and
collision charges is finite, with per-family bound
\[
  (2^h-1)+\frac{h(h-1)}2,
\]
for bad parameters.  The explicit multiplier ledger attached to each primitive
denominator is needed only when counting certificate witnesses: after base
endpoint collision charges, cancelling a multiplier leaves the same primitive
denominator with the same \(\lambda\) or \(\theta\), and a second parameter for
that primitive denominator would itself be a charged collision.  This packages
the geometry behind the endpoint-short route; it does not improve the direct
\(|\Lambda_{K,>b}|,|\Theta_{D,>b}|\le h\) closure bound that already follows
from short injectivity.

In the ruled part, the common-kernel branch has a fixed projective boundary slope,
while the common-image branch forces the shadow locator \(L_S\) to satisfy the
lower and upper row-cut equations obtained by contracting both Hankel windows
against the annihilator of the common image line.  Thus the boundary residual
is split into a unique-neighbor shadow problem, low-exchange active-codegree
ledgers, and a row-cut resonance problem.

The common-kernel branch sharpens further: if its kernel relation has finite
slope \(z\), then both shifted shadow locators \(\ell_S^0,\ell_S^+\) lie in the
same fixed-slope kernel of \(H(u)+zH(v)\), so the entire external-anchor pencil
is a fixed-slope boundary slice.  The remaining endpoint case is
\(H(v)\ell_S^0=H(v)\ell_S^+=0\), a boundary-contained pencil.

For the common-image row-cut side, full shadow stars are also not new
obstructions: a row cut on degree-\((j-1)\) shadow locators containing the full
star through \(\alpha\in D\) must be the evaluation cut \(L_S(\alpha)=0\).
After these full stars are moved to the fixed-root/root-slice ledger, a
star-free rank-one row-cut packet gains the slice factor
\((j-2)/(j-1)\) over the bare one-root-loss bound.

In the polynomial-field window \(q=|\F|\le n^{B_F}\), this gives a concrete
closure criterion.  After fixed-root/full-star boundary slices have been
charged, if the reduced shadow ledgers for the nondegenerate quadratic branch,
finite common-kernel fixed-slope branch, boundary-contained endpoint branch,
and common-image row-cut branch are polynomially bounded, then
\[
|\mathrm{Boundary}_{\mathrm{off}}^{\mathrm{red}}|
 \le 2|\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathrm{quad}}|
  + q\bigl(|\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathrm{ker}}|
          +|\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathrm{cont}}|
          +|\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathrm{img}}|\bigr)
\]
is polynomial.  This does not prove those shadow-ledger estimates; it records
that the remaining boundary-off multiplicity is at worst the field-size factor
over the three ruled shadow images and a factor \(2\) over the nondegenerate
shadow image.

The common-image shadow ledger is now rank-testable.  For each projective image
line \(I\subset \F^t\), the lower/upper Hankel contractions define an affine
row-cut system on monic degree-\((j-1)\) shadow locators.  If its direction
dimension is \(d_I\), then the number of squarefree \(D\)-split shadows in that
fixed image-line stratum is at most \(\binom{n}{d_I}\).  Hence
\[
|\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathrm{img}}|
  \le \sum_I \binom{n}{d_I}.
\]
In bounded slack and polynomial field size, the common-image branch is therefore
closed by bounded direction dimension; the only remaining obstruction in this
branch is the low-rank image-line locus.

The finite common-kernel and endpoint-contained ruled ledgers admit the same
rank certificate.  For a finite slope \(z\), the conditions
\((H(u)+zH(v))\ell_S^0=(H(u)+zH(v))\ell_S^+=0\) cut out an affine row-cut
system on monic degree-\((j-1)\) shadow locators; if its direction dimension is
\(d_z\), then that fixed-slope shadow stratum has size at most
\(\binom{n}{d_z}\).  The endpoint branch is the same statement with \(H(v)\)
and dimension \(d_\infty\).  Thus the whole ruled part of the reduced
boundary-off ledger is bounded by
\[
q\left(\sum_z\binom{n}{d_z}+\binom{n}{d_\infty}
       +\sum_I\binom{n}{d_I}\right),
\]
so every ruled branch is now expressed as an explicit Hankel row-rank ledger.
For finite slopes this low-rank locus has a one-variable determinantal
dichotomy: fixing a threshold \(b<m=j-1\), either the set of finite slopes with
direction dimension \(d_z>b\) has size at most \(m-b\), or every
\((m-b)\times(m-b)\) minor of the row-cut matrix vanishes identically in \(z\).
Thus a large finite-slope low-rank obstruction must come from a persistent
low-rank Hankel pencil, not from many unrelated exceptional slopes.
Equivalently, at any fixed threshold \(b\), if the persistent alternative is
absent and the at most \(m-b\) exceptional slopes are charged to the
fixed-slope/root-slice ledger, then the bounded-rank finite common-kernel
residual contributes at most
\[
q^2\binom{n}{b}.
\]
For bounded \(b\) and \(q\le n^{B_F}\), this closes that residual
polynomially and leaves only the explicit exceptional fixed-slope charge.
The identically persistent alternative is also certificate-form: over the
rational function field \(\F(z)\), vanishing of every
\((m-b)\times(m-b)\) minor is equivalent to the existence of \(b+1\)
independent polynomial vectors \(Q_i(z,X)\), \(\deg_X Q_i<m\), with
\[
(H(u)+zH(v))\ell_{Q_i(z)}^0=(H(u)+zH(v))\ell_{Q_i(z)}^+=0
\]
as polynomial identities in \(z\).  The remaining persistent case is therefore
an explicit moving-kernel certificate to classify or rule out.
Moreover, persistence forces endpoint low rank: the constant and degree-\(r\)
coefficients of the \(r\times r\) determinantal minors show that the \(H(u)\)
and \(H(v)\) endpoint row-cut systems both have direction dimension \(>b\),
where \(r=m-b\).  Equivalently, if either endpoint has direction dimension at
most \(b\), the persistent alternative is impossible and the finite-exception
closure applies.  A moving kernel \(Q(z)=\sum q_i z^i\) also gives the ladder
\[
R_uq_0=0,\qquad R_uq_i+R_vq_{i-1}=0,\qquad R_vq_D=0,
\]
so persistent finite-slope degeneracy starts and ends in endpoint kernels.
The common-image low-rank locus has an analogous projective form.  For an
image line \(I=[y]\subset\F^t\), the direction space is the kernel of
\[
Q\longmapsto
\bigl(y\wedge H(u)\ell_Q^0,\ y\wedge H(u)\ell_Q^+,
      y\wedge H(v)\ell_Q^0,\ y\wedge H(v)\ell_Q^+\bigr).
\]
Thus the locus \(d_I>b\) is cut out in \(\mathbb P^{t-1}\) by homogeneous
\((m-b)\times(m-b)\) minors of degree \(m-b\).  Outside this projective
low-rank locus, the common-image contribution is bounded by
\[
q\,\frac{q^t-1}{q-1}\binom{n}{b},
\]
while the fully persistent case is an explicit moving-image wedge certificate.
Combining the finite-slope, endpoint, and common-image estimates gives the
ruled closure ledger: after charging finite slopes with \(d_z>b\), projective
image lines with \(d_I>b\), and assuming \(d_\infty\le b\), the remaining ruled
targets are bounded by
\[
\left(q^2+q+q\,\frac{q^t-1}{q-1}\right)\binom{n}{b}.
\]
Thus bounded \(b,t\) and polynomial field size close the bounded-rank ruled
residual; what remains is only the explicit fixed-slope, projective low-rank,
or moving-kernel certificate loci.

There is also a root-free sharpening of these row-rank ledgers.  Let
\(m=j-1\) and let \(P_0+V\) be one affine row-rank stratum with
\(\dim V=d\).  After common-root subledgers have been charged to the
fixed-root/root-slice ledger, incidence counting through roots gives
\[
|\mathrm{Sh}(P_0+V)|\le \frac{n}{m}\binom{n-1}{d-1}
  =\frac{d}{m}\binom{n}{d}
\]
for \(d\ge1\), with the old bound \(1\) kept when \(d=0\).  Thus in the
bounded-rank range \(1\le b\le n/2\), the root-free ruled closure replaces the
bare factor \(\binom{n}{b}\) above by \((b/m)\binom{n}{b}\).  This does not
remove the exceptional low-rank loci, but it shows that the bounded-rank
residual carries a genuine fixed-root saving once common-root pieces are
charged.

\textbf{Status.}  This is a proved local packet lemma under the Hankel-pencil
normal form and its non-fixed variable-line hypotheses.  It is not the final
all-line M1 theorem and does not add a leaderboard row.

\section{Towards-Prize Finite-Threshold Theorems}
\label{sec:towards-prize-thresholds}

\textbf{Status: \textsc{New-local} finite theorem infrastructure plus
subsumed strict264 proof record.}
The results in this section are deliberately finite and certificate-facing.  They
are meant to move the experimental ledger from lower-bound numerators toward the
integer agreement staircase needed by the Proximity Prize.  They do not prove the
corrected Paper B local-limit conjecture.  The later
high-agreement tangent staircase supersedes the old strict264/265 search target
as a threshold-pinning route for the \(\F_{17^{32}}\), \(n=512\), \(k=256\)
row; the strict264 material below remains useful as certificate language and
as a record of quotient-core mechanisms.

For a received line \(\ell_z=f+zg\), write
\[
  \operatorname{Bad}_{f,g}(C,a)
\]
for the set of finite slopes \(z\in F\) for which there is a support
\(S\subseteq D\), \(|S|\ge a\), and a codeword \(c\in C\) satisfying
\(c|_S=(f+zg)|_S\), but there is no pair of codewords
\(c_f,c_g\in C\) with \(c_f|_S=f|_S\) and \(c_g|_S=g|_S\).  Put
\[
  \operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}^{f,g}(C,a)
  =|\operatorname{Bad}_{f,g}(C,a)|,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}(C,a)
  =\max_{f,g}\operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}^{f,g}(C,a),
\]
where the maximum is over the line family allowed by the sampler under
consideration.  The field paying the denominator is always \(\qline\), not
silently \(\qgen\) or \(\qchal\).

\begin{proposition}[certificate-to-\(\operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}\) gate]
\label{prop:certificate-to-ldsw}
Let \(C=\RS[F,D,k]\), let \(a\ge k\), and fix a received line \(f+zg\).  Suppose
there is a finite set \(\mathcal C\) of tuples
\[
  (z_i,S_i,P_i),
  \qquad z_i\in F,
  \quad S_i\subseteq D,
  \quad |S_i|\ge a,
  \quad P_i\in F[X]_{<k},
\]
with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the slopes \(z_i\) are pairwise distinct;
\item \(P_i(x)=f(x)+z_i g(x)\) for every \(x\in S_i\);
\item there are no \(P_{i,f},P_{i,g}\in F[X]_{<k}\) satisfying
  \(P_{i,f}|_{S_i}=f|_{S_i}\) and \(P_{i,g}|_{S_i}=g|_{S_i}\).
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
  \operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}^{f,g}(C,a)\ge |\mathcal C|,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}(C,a)\ge |\mathcal C|.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each tuple certifies that the slope \(z_i\) is explained on a support of size at
least \(a\), and the third condition is exactly support-wise noncontainment on
that support.  Pairwise distinctness of the slopes prevents multiple tuples from
paying for the same bad slope.  Thus all \(z_i\)'s lie in
\(\operatorname{Bad}_{f,g}(C,a)\), giving the first inequality.  The second
follows from the definition of \(\operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}(C,a)\) as a maximum
over lines.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[strict264 certificate target]
\label{cor:strict264-certificate-target}
For the row
\[
  C=\RS[\F_{17^{32}},H,256],
  \qquad |H|=512,
\]
a strict264 certificate consists of seven tuples in
Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate-to-ldsw} with \(a=264\).  Such a certificate
proves
\[
  \operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}(C,264)\ge 7.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate-to-ldsw} with
\(|\mathcal C|=7\) and \(a=264\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[fixed-locator unique-slope principle]
\label{prop:fixed-locator-unique-slope}
Use the notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:syndrome-pencil}.  Fix
\(a\ge k\), put \(t=a-k\), \(j=n-a=r-t\), and fix a squarefree
\(D\)-split locator \(L_T\) of degree \(j\).  Let
\[
  A_T=H_{t,j}(u)\boldsymbol\ell_T,
  \qquad
  B_T=H_{t,j}(v)\boldsymbol\ell_T.
\]
If \(B_T\ne0\), then this locator contributes at most one finite
support-wise noncontained slope.  More precisely, a slope exists exactly when
\(A_T\) is a scalar multiple of \(B_T\), in which case the unique slope is
\[
  z_T=-A_{T,m}/B_{T,m}
\]
for any coordinate \(m\) with \(B_{T,m}\ne0\), and the same value must be
obtained from every nonzero coordinate of \(B_T\).
Consequently
\[
  \operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}^{f,g}(C,a)
  \le
  \#\{T\subset D:
        |T|=n-a,
        L_T\text{ squarefree and }D\text{-split},
        B_T\ne0,
        A_T\in F B_T\}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the fixed locator, Theorem~\ref{thm:syndrome-pencil} gives the incidence
equation
\[
  A_T+zB_T=0
\]
and the noncontainment condition \(B_T\ne0\).  If \(B_T\ne0\), any solution
\(z\) is forced by any coordinate where \(B_T\) is nonzero.  Existence is exactly
the consistency condition that all coordinates give the same scalar, equivalently
\(A_T\in F B_T\).  Counting possible locators gives the final bound; different
locators may still collide to the same slope, so the displayed quantity is an
upper bound on distinct slopes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[why this helps the 265 upper bound]
For the current \(n=512,k=256\) row, this proposition remains useful for
duplicate-aware locator searches.  The old proposed upper bound
\(\operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}(C,265)\le6\) is now known to be false by
Theorem~\ref{thm:moving-root-tangent-floor}, which already gives
\(\operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}(C,265)\ge248\).  Future upper-bound searches should
therefore start beyond the exact tangent gate, at agreement \(507\) and higher,
or explicitly isolate non-tangent mechanisms.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[subfield confinement for base-valued lines]
\label{thm:subfield-confinement-base-lines}
Let \(K\subseteq F\) be a subfield, let \(D\subset K\), and let
\(C_F=\RS[F,D,k]\).  Suppose \(f,g:D\to K\), and let
\(S\subseteq D\) have \(|S|\ge k\).  If \(z\in F\setminus K\) and there is a
polynomial \(P\in F[X]_{<k}\) with
\[
  P(x)=f(x)+zg(x)
  \qquad (x\in S),
\]
then there are polynomials \(P_f,P_g\in K[X]_{<k}\) such that
\[
  P_f|_S=f|_S,
  \qquad
  P_g|_S=g|_S.
\]
Consequently, for \(K\)-valued received lines and agreement \(a\ge k\), every
support-wise noncontained finite slope lies in \(K\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a subset \(S_0\subseteq S\) of size \(k\).  Interpolate the values of
\(f\) and \(g\) on \(S_0\) to obtain unique polynomials
\(P_f,P_g\in K[X]_{<k}\).  On \(S_0\), the polynomial
\(P_f+zP_g\) agrees with \(P\).  Since both have degree less than \(k\) and agree
on \(k\) points, they are equal as polynomials over \(F\).  For every
\(x\in S\), therefore,
\[
  0=f(x)+zg(x)-P(x)
   =(f(x)-P_f(x))+z(g(x)-P_g(x)).
\]
Both coefficients in the final expression lie in \(K\).  Since
\(z\notin K\), the elements \(1,z\) are linearly independent over \(K\).  Hence
both coefficients vanish for every \(x\in S\), proving the separate explanations
of \(f\) and \(g\).  Thus any explained slope outside \(K\) is support-wise
contained, so a noncontained slope must lie in \(K\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[extension-field warning]
Theorem~\ref{thm:subfield-confinement-base-lines} is intentionally one-sided.
It only treats \(K\)-valued received lines.  It does not rule out genuinely
\(F\)-valued residue-line witnesses, and therefore must not be used as an
extension-field MCA theorem without an additional hypothesis forcing the line to
be \(K\)-valued.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[seven-slope arithmetic gate over \(\F_{17^{32}}\)]
\label{thm:seven-slope-arithmetic-gate}
Let \(\qline=17^{32}\).  If a row over \(\F_{17^{32}}\) has
\(\operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}(C,264)\ge7\), then at radius
\[
  \delta=1-\frac{264}{512}=\frac{31}{64}
\]
its support-wise MCA error is strictly larger than \(2^{-128}\).  In particular,
for the \(n=512,k=256\) row, any strict264 seven-slope certificate proves
\[
  \varepsilon_{\rm mca}(C,31/64)>2^{-128}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The MCA sampler over finite slopes has denominator \(\qline=17^{32}\).  Seven
bad slopes therefore give error at least \(7/17^{32}\).  The exact integer
comparison is
\[
  7\cdot 2^{128}-17^{32}
  =14064973686101998399515954701426232831>0.
\]
Thus \(7/17^{32}>2^{-128}\).  Agreement \(264\) on a domain of size \(512\)
corresponds to relative radius \(1-264/512=31/64\), giving the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[one-step staircase pinning criterion]
\label{thm:one-step-staircase}
Fix a finite-slope sampler of denominator \(\qline\) and a target
\(\varepsilon_*=2^{-128}\).  Let
\[
  B(a)=\operatorname{LD}_{\rm sw}(C,a)
\]
for the allowed line family, and let \(\delta_a=1-a/n\).  If, for some integer
\(a\ge k\),
\[
  B(a)>\varepsilon_*\qline
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  B(a+1)\le \varepsilon_*\qline,
\]
then the closed-ball agreement staircase is unsafe at radius \(\delta_a\) and
safe on the next open interval below it, including the grid radius
\(\delta_{a+1}\).  Equivalently, the largest grid radius certified safe is
\(\delta_{a+1}\), while the continuous supremum of certified safe radii is
\(\delta_a\) and is not attained under the closed endpoint convention.

Before the tangent staircase was isolated, the special case
\[
  B(264)\ge7,
  \qquad
  B(265)\le6
\]
would have pinned the finite row to an unsafe/safe transition between
\[
  \delta_{265}=247/512
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \delta_{264}=31/64.
\]
For the \(\F_{17^{32}}\), \(n=512\), \(k=256\) row, the premise
\(B(265)\le6\) is false by the moving-root tangent floor.  The actual
finite-slope staircase gate supplied by Corollary~\ref{cor:f17-32-exact-tangent-gate}
is \(B(506)=7\), \(B(507)=6\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The function \(B(a)\) is nonincreasing in \(a\), because raising the agreement
threshold only removes valid supports.  At radius \(\delta_a\), the closed-ball
predicate allows agreement at least \(a\), so the error is \(B(a)/\qline\), which
is larger than \(\varepsilon_*\) by assumption.  At radius \(\delta_{a+1}\), the
predicate requires agreement at least \(a+1\), so the error is
\(B(a+1)/\qline\), which is at most \(\varepsilon_*\).

More generally, any radius \(\delta<\delta_a\) sufficiently close to
\(\delta_a\) has integer distance bound at most \(n-a-1\), hence agreement at
least \(a+1\).  Thus the closed endpoint at \(\delta_a\) is the first unsafe
grid step, while the safe side is immediately below that endpoint.  For
\(\qline=17^{32}\), the inequalities
\[
  6\cdot2^{128}<17^{32}<7\cdot2^{128}
\]
convert the printed integer conditions into the two displayed error inequalities,
and \(1-265/512=247/512\), \(1-264/512=31/64\).
\end{proof}
