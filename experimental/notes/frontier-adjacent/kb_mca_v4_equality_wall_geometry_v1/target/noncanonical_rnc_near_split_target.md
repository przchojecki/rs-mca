# Noncanonical Rational-Normal Near-Split Target

## 1. Role and status

The complete overloaded-line branch is now proved impossible in every
relevant KoalaBear row. The grouped-Cauchy component theorem includes
common selected roots, proportional coordinate factors, and the generic
diagonal-Cauchy case.

The following are already proved:

1. every degree-\(k\) relation has support at least \(k+2\);
2. every relation of support at most \(2k+1\) contains \(k+2\)
   collinear selected vertices;
3. canonical block lines have an exact full/near-full split-fiber
   capacity;
4. canonical lines cannot reach the relation threshold at
   \((a,R)=(12,69)\) or \(a=14,\ R=67,68,69\);
5. every known finite postcritical relation space is generated by
   canonical block-line relations;
6. a generic noncanonical line cannot reach the overload threshold at
   \((a,R)=(12,69)\) or \(a=14,\ R=67,68,69\);
7. after grouping proportional coordinate factors and removing all
   common selected roots, the effective source-pole period excludes the
   two formerly open structural branches as well.

Items 6 and 7 are proved in
`postcritical_diagonal_cauchy_periodicity_reduction.md` and
`postcritical_grouped_cauchy_component_reduction.md`. This note does not
handle conic, cubic, or support-at-least-\(3k+1\) relations.

## 2. Exact input

Let

\[
n=a-1,\qquad k=R-a+2.
\]

Fix distinct source values

\[
\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_a
\]

and distinct selected values

\[
t_1,\ldots,t_R,
\]

disjoint from the source set.

Let \(\ell\subseteq\mathbf P^{a-1}\) be a noncanonical line containing
\(m\) reciprocal-Cauchy vertices

\[
y_I=
\left[
\frac1{P_I(\alpha_1)}:\cdots:
\frac1{P_I(\alpha_a)}
\right],
\qquad
P_I(T)=\prod_{t\in I}(T-t),
\qquad
|I|=n,
\]

with

\[
m\ge k+2=R-a+4.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The line is noncanonical: it is not \(L_B\) for any selected
\(a\)-block \(B\).

## 3. Exact bivariate normal form

Choose affine line coordinates

\[
z_j(\lambda)=u_j+\lambda v_j.
\]

With \(L_j(T)\) the source Lagrange basis, put

\[
\Delta(\lambda)=\prod_{j=1}^a z_j(\lambda)
\]

and

\[
N(T,\lambda)
=
\sum_{j=1}^a
L_j(T)\frac{\Delta(\lambda)}{z_j(\lambda)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
\deg_TN\le n,\qquad
\deg_\lambda N\le n.
\]

If

\[
D(\lambda)=[T^n]N(T,\lambda),
\]

every selected vertex on \(\ell\) has a distinct parameter \(\lambda_s\)
with

\[
D(\lambda_s)\ne0
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
P_{I_s}(T)=N(T,\lambda_s)/D(\lambda_s).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus

\[
N(t_i,\lambda_s)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t_i\in I_s.
\tag{3.3}
\]

## 4. Already separated structural branches

### 4.1 Common selected root

If

\[
N(t_i,\lambda)\equiv0
\]

for some selected \(t_i\), then every line locator has the common factor
\((T-t_i)\). This is a rooted common-factor/quotient precursor.

### 4.2 Coordinate-ratio rank defect

If two projective factors

\[
[u_j:v_j]=[u_r:v_r],
\]

then the line has a constant coordinate ratio and the coefficient span of

\[
\prod_{h\ne j}(u_h+\lambda v_h)
\]

has rank less than \(a\). This is an exact collective-rank/coordinate
precursor.

### 4.3 Generic rational-normal branch

If all \([u_j:v_j]\) are distinct, the polynomials

\[
g_j(\lambda)=\prod_{h\ne j}(u_h+\lambda v_h)
\]

form a basis of \(F[\lambda]_{\le n}\), and

\[
t\longmapsto[N(t,\lambda)]
\]

is a degree-\(n\) rational normal curve in
\(\mathbf P(F[\lambda]_{\le n})\).

Because the original line is noncanonical, distinct locator supports
satisfy

\[
|I_s\cap I_{s'}|\le n-2=a-3.
\tag{4.1}
\]

## 7. Resolved generic algebraic core

Historically, the generic branch asked for an inverse theorem for a
special full-rank bidegree-\((n,n)\) polynomial. The periodicity argument
in Section 7.5 now resolves this branch. The preceding formulations are
retained because they identify the exact algebraic object and provide
useful regressions for any alternative proof. Put

\[
A_\Sigma(T)=\prod_{j=1}^a(T-\alpha_j).
\]

After moving the roots of the distinct line factors into one affine
chart, write

\[
\ell_j(\lambda)=c_j(\lambda-\beta_j),
\qquad
B(\lambda)=\prod_{j=1}^a(\lambda-\beta_j).
\]

The exact Lagrange formula gives

\[
\boxed{
N(T,\lambda)
=
A_\Sigma(T)B(\lambda)
\sum_{j=1}^a
\frac{w_j}
{(T-\alpha_j)(\lambda-\beta_j)},
\qquad w_j\ne0.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus, away from the source and line poles,

\[
\boxed{
N(t,\lambda)=0
\iff
x(t)^\mathsf TWy(\lambda)=0,
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

where

\[
x(t)=((t-\alpha_j)^{-1})_j,\qquad
y(\lambda)=((\lambda-\beta_j)^{-1})_j,\qquad
W=\operatorname{diag}(w_j).
\]

Both \(x(t)\) and \(y(\lambda)\) are rational normal curves and \(W\) is
nondegenerate. The remaining theorem is therefore a diagonal
Cauchy-pairing incidence theorem for two rational normal curves. It is
strictly narrower than a theorem for arbitrary full-rank bidegree forms.

It has \(m\) vertical fibers that split into \(n\) roots from one fixed
\(R\)-point set, while every horizontal fiber contains at most \(n\) of
the selected \(\lambda\)-parameters. Excessive incidence must force one
of:

1. a common vertical factor;
2. coefficient-rank loss;
3. a Möbius-orbit, quotient, or finite-field correspondence;
4. a bounded repeated split template;
5. an impossible incidence count.

This formulation excludes the source-free arbitrary-mask problem. It
retains the exact reciprocal-Cauchy coupling through the invertible
coefficient matrix and the two rational normal curves.

### 7.1 Dual-GRS near-minimum formulation

Evaluate \(N_t(\lambda)=N(t,\lambda)\) at the \(m\) actual line
parameters. These evaluation vectors belong to the same
\([m,a,m-a+1]\) generalized Reed--Solomon code. If \(r_i\) is the root
multiplicity from Section 5, then

\[
\operatorname{wt}(N_{t_i})
=m-r_i
=(m-a+1)+(a-1-r_i).
\tag{7.3}
\]

At minimum overload their total excess above the MDS minimum is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^R
\left(
\operatorname{wt}(N_{t_i})-(m-a+1)
\right)
=(a-4)(a-1).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

For \(a=12,R=69,m=61\), the packet contains 69 codewords of one
\([61,12,50]\) GRS code. Their coefficient points lie on the selected
rational normal curve, their total excess weight is 88, and at least 25
have weight at most 51.

Before the periodicity proof, this suggested the sharper generic
subtarget:

> **RNC-constrained near-minimum GRS theorem.** A nondegenerate diagonal
> Cauchy pairing of the two actual source curves cannot produce the
> packet in (5.4), unless it factors through a common root, coordinate
> ratio, Möbius correspondence, or bounded repeated split template.

The rational-normal constraint on the coefficient points is
load-bearing. Ordinary GRS weight enumeration alone permits many
near-minimum codewords.

### 7.2 Higher-order intersections

For any \(d\) distinct line locators, rational-normal linear
independence gives

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\bigcap_{s=1}^d I_s
\right|
\le a-d.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The genuine noncanonical condition improves the \(d=2\) case from
\(a-2\) to \(a-3\). Equality for \(d\ge3\) prints a common split factor
of degree \(a-d\) and a complete degree-\((d-1)\) quotient section. Such
equality cases should be routed as bounded planted/quotient templates
before treating the strict-intersection remainder.

### 7.3 Degree-88 resultant remainder

At minimum overload, put

\[
L(\lambda)=\prod_{s=1}^m(\lambda-\lambda_s),
\qquad
\mathcal R(\lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^R N(t_i,\lambda).
\]

Every \(\lambda_s\) contributes its \(a-1\) selected roots, so

\[
L^{a-1}\mid\mathcal R
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\deg(\mathcal R/L^{a-1})
\le(a-4)(a-1).
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

The hardest packet has one residual polynomial of degree at most 88.
This is the same quantity as the total GRS weight excess, but its
factorization retains multiplicities and can be compared directly with
the diagonal-Cauchy determinant.

### 7.4 Two-anchor split-or-quotient law

For any two anchor locators \(P_0,P_\infty\), every third locator obeys

\[
\boxed{
P_\lambda
(r_\lambda P_\infty+s_\lambda P_0)
-
c_\lambda P_0P_\infty
=
A_\Sigma Q_\lambda,
\qquad
\deg Q_\lambda\le a-2.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Consequently either

\[
|I_\lambda\cap(I_0\cup I_\infty)|\le a-2,
\tag{7.8}
\]

or \(Q_\lambda=0\) and \(I_\lambda\subseteq I_0\cup I_\infty\), an exact
two-anchor split template. At equality in (7.8), \(Q_\lambda\) is the
split locator of the shared roots.

This removes one more apparent source of arbitrary behavior. A proof
should first route the zero-quotient and split-boundary cases, then work
with the strict two-anchor overlap inequality.

### 7.5 Source-pole periodicity and generic exclusion

Let

\[
\mathcal D=V(N)\subseteq\mathbf P^1_T\times\mathbf P^1_\lambda.
\]

The diagonal-Cauchy form gives the exact line-bundle period

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}(a,-a)\simeq\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}.
}
\tag{7.9}
\]

The restrictions of \(A_\Sigma(T)\) and \(B(\lambda)\) have the same
Cartier divisor: the \(a(a-1)\) off-diagonal points
\((\alpha_i,\beta_j)\), \(i\ne j\).

The selected grid creates a nonzero section

\[
0\ne\sigma_{\rm grid}
\in H^0\!\left(
\mathcal D,\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}(R,-m)
\right).
\tag{7.10}
\]

After applying (7.9), the relevant rows reduce to

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
(a,R)&m\text{ range}&
\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}(R,-m)\text{ reduced form}\\ \hline
(12,69)&61\le m\le69&
\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}(9,-(m-60))\\
(14,67)&57\le m\le67&
\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}(11,-(m-56))\\
(14,68)&58\le m\le68&
\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}(12,-(m-56))\\
(14,69)&59\le m\le69&
\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}(13,-(m-56)).
\end{array}
\tag{7.11}
\]

For a bidegree-\((n,n)\) divisor,

\[
H^0(\mathcal D,\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}(r,-s))=0
\qquad(0\le r\le n-1,\ s\ge1).
\tag{7.12}
\]

This eliminates the first three rows. In the last row \(r=n=13\).
Writing

\[
N(T,\lambda)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}N_j(T)\lambda^j,
\]

the generic diagonal-Cauchy condition makes the \(N_j\) a basis of
\(F[T]_{\le n}\). The induced Laurent-convolution map

\[
H^1(\mathcal O(0,-s-n))
\xrightarrow{\times N}
H^1(\mathcal O(n,-s))
\]

is injective for every \(s\ge2\), so the same vanishing holds. Here
\(s=m-56\ge3\).

Thus the section (7.10) cannot exist in the generic branch. The
61-record \([61,12,50]\) GRS packet and its degree-88 resultant
remainder are inconsistent, rather than a surviving inverse problem.

## 8. Historical routes

### 8.1 Bivariate GM--MDS minor

Choose \(n+1\) vertical fibers and interpolate \(N\) in \(\lambda\).
Every further split fiber gives \(n\) prescribed zeros. Prove that a
canonical \((n+1)^2\)-constraint minor is nonzero unless its edge pattern
contains a common factor or repeated split template. Specialization to the
actual KoalaBear field must be certified; a formal-root determinant alone
is insufficient.

The diagonal form (7.1) reduces the candidate determinant to a Cauchy
minor with diagonal weights. This is the preferred exactification: prove
that every zero-pattern minor at the overload threshold either has a
nonzero Cauchy determinant or prints one of the allowed factorization
patterns.

The periodicity theorem supersedes this route for the generic branch in
the KoalaBear rows.

### 8.2 Rational-normal low-weight intersection

The points

\[
[N(t_i,\lambda)]
\]

lie on a rational normal curve in \(\mathbf P^n\). At least the number in
(5.3) lie in the union of projective lines of polynomials divisible by a
fixed degree-\((n-1)\) locator on the \(\lambda_s\). Classify a rational
normal curve meeting this split-locator arrangement excessively.

Equivalently, classify rational-normal subcurves of the near-minimum
weight locus of one \([m,a,m-a+1]\) GRS code. The total-excess identity
(7.4), rather than only the number of low-weight words, should be used.

This interpretation remains useful, but the generic near-minimum packet
is already excluded by (7.9)-(7.12).

### 8.3 Correspondence factorization

Study the curve

\[
N(T,\lambda)=0
\subseteq\mathbf P^1_T\times\mathbf P^1_\lambda.
\]

If it is reducible, its factors should print lower-degree quotient or
Möbius correspondences. If it is irreducible, use the two degree-\(n\)
projections and the near-saturated grid incidence to bound the number of
completely split selected fibers.

No irreducibility split is needed for the generic packing theorem: the
Cartier-divisor and cohomology argument applies to the complete
bidegree-\((n,n)\) divisor.

### 8.4 Repeated-root graph

The bipartite incidence graph has left degree \(n\), right degree at most
\(n\), and edge deficit (5.2). Combine the pair-intersection cap (4.1)
with the polynomial identities for repeated \(2\times2\) rectangles.
Combinatorics alone is not expected to suffice, but it can isolate a
bounded family of algebraic rectangle types.

Use the higher-order law (7.5), not just pair intersections. First peel
off equality cases, which already carry a common split quotient. In the
strict remainder, combine the intersection deficits with the degree-88
factor in (7.6).

The two-anchor law (7.7) provides a second peeling rule: any third block
with more than \(a-2\) roots in the anchor union is already a complete
two-anchor template. Equality gives a printed degree-\((a-2)\) quotient.

### 8.5 Retired existing-owner adapter route

Before the grouped-Cauchy theorem, one possible continuation was to
translate the common factor or coordinate-ratio defect back through the
source-partition Cremona identities. That adapter would have needed to
identify the same regular record and instantiate one of the seven active
KoalaBear owner predicates. The effective-period argument now excludes
both branches directly, so this semantic route is unnecessary.

## 9. Separate higher-support obligations

The published minimal-linked-point classification recorded in
`postcritical_cayley_bacharach_curve_reduction.md` sharpens this branch.
Every minimal degree-\(k\) relation of support at most \(3k\) is exactly
one of:

1. \(k+2\) collinear points;
2. \(2k+2\) points on a plane conic, with no \(k+2\) collinear;
3. \(3k\) coplanar points forming a cubic/degree-\(k\) complete
   intersection.

Thus full SPSI needs two additional statements:

> **Selected-record low-degree-curve emission.** Every actual conic or
> cubic circuit above emits an enabled same-record semantic cell.

> **Postcritical large-circuit reduction.** Every nonzero postcritical
> relation either contains a circuit of size at most \(3k\), or a
> circuit of size at least \(3k+1\) directly emits an enabled
> same-record semantic cell.

At \(a=12,R=69\), the exact circuit sizes are 61, 120, and 177. The
unclassified circuit range therefore begins at 178, rather than 120.

The reducible 120-point conic is especially concrete: it consists of two
60-point lines. Since the canonical line capacity is exactly 60, the
hard boundary is a pair of individually subcritical saturated lines.
The irreducible conic has an exact quadratic-coordinate locator normal
form and a resultant remainder of degree at most 198. These statements
are proved in the cited reduction note.
