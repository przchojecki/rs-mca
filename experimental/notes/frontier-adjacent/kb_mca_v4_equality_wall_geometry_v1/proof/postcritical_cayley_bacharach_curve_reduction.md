# Postcritical Cayley--Bacharach Curve Reduction

## 1. Status

This note extends the postcritical relation classification beyond the
low-support line range.

It consumes the minimal-linked-point theorem of A. Couvreur,
*The dual minimum distance of arbitrary dimensional algebraic--geometric
codes*, J. Algebra 350 (2012), 84--107, Theorem 3.8 and Propositions
7.2, 7.4, 8.1, and 9.1:

```text
https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.2345
```

The complete line branch is now excluded by
`postcritical_grouped_cauchy_component_reduction.md`. The irreducible
conic branch is further reduced by
`postcritical_conic_pole_support_reduction.md` to one pole-disjoint
fixed-domain design in the principal row. The cubic and large-circuit
branches remain open.

## 2. Minimal postcritical relations

Let

\[
Y\subseteq\mathbf P^{a-1}(F)
\]

be the selected reciprocal-Cauchy configuration, and put

\[
k=R-a+2.
\]

A degree-\(k\) relation is a nonzero vector

\[
\lambda=(\lambda_y)_{y\in Y},
\qquad
\sum_{y\in Y}\lambda_y f(y)=0
\]

for every degree-\(k\) form \(f\).

Every relation contains an inclusion-minimal nonzero subrelation:
choose a nonempty support minimal under inclusion. Its support \(C\) is
a circuit of the degree-\(k\) evaluation matroid, equivalently a
minimally \(k\)-linked set in Couvreur's terminology.

Scalar extension to an algebraic closure preserves the relation,
minimality, and support. Couvreur's theorem therefore applies.

## 3. Exact trichotomy through support \(3k\)

### Theorem 3.1

Let \(C\) support a minimally degree-\(k\) relation.

1. If \(|C|\le2k+1\), then
   \[
   \boxed{|C|=k+2}
   \]
   and the points of \(C\) are collinear.

2. If \(|C|\le3k-1\) and the first case does not occur, then
   \[
   \boxed{|C|=2k+2}
   \]
   and the points of \(C\) lie on a plane conic.

3. If \(|C|=3k\) and neither previous case occurs, then \(C\) is
   coplanar and is the complete intersection of a cubic and a
   degree-\(k\) plane curve with no common component.

### Justification

Couvreur proves:

* any \(k\)-linked set with at most \(2k+1\) points contains \(k+2\)
  collinear points;
* any \(k\)-linked set with at most \(3k-1\), with no \(k+2\)
  collinear points, contains \(2k+2\) points on a plane conic;
* the first remaining minimally linked configuration has \(3k\) points
  and is the stated complete intersection.

Each displayed subset is itself \(k\)-linked. Minimality of \(C\)
therefore forces \(C\) to equal that subset, giving the exact support
sizes above.

## 4. Hardest KoalaBear arithmetic

For

\[
a=12,\qquad R=69,\qquad k=59,
\]

the three possible circuit sizes are

\[
\boxed{
61,\qquad120,\qquad177.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently, every hypothetical postcritical relation has one of four
outcomes:

1. it contains a 61-point line circuit;
2. it contains a 120-point plane-conic circuit;
3. it contains a 177-point cubic complete-intersection circuit; or
4. every circuit contained in its support has size at least 178.

The first outcome is the line branch treated in
`postcritical_low_support_line_reduction.md` and proved impossible in
`postcritical_grouped_cauchy_component_reduction.md`.

Thus the previously stated “support greater than 119” obligation can be
replaced by the sharper list

\[
\boxed{
\text{conic emission}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\text{cubic-complete-intersection emission}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\text{circuit support at least }178.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

## 5. Reducible conic branch

Suppose the conic is \(L_1\cup L_2\). The circuit has \(2k+2\) distinct
points and contains no \(k+2\) collinear points. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
|C\cap L_1|=|C\cap L_2|=k+1,
\qquad
C\cap L_1\cap L_2=\varnothing.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Indeed, each line contains at most \(k+1\) circuit points, while their
union contains \(2k+2\).

At \(a=12,R=69\), this is a pair of 60-point lines. The exact canonical
line capacity is also 60. Hence the reducible-conic branch has a sharp
boundary configuration:

\[
\boxed{
\text{two individually subcritical 60-point lines whose union carries
one degree-59 relation.}
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

If both lines are canonical and attain capacity, each is generated by
five disjoint selected 12-blocks. This prints a bounded two-pencil,
ten-block source template. If either line is noncanonical, it is one
point below the proved overload threshold. The complete line theorem
does not exclude this saturated pair, so a paired-line or planted
template argument is still required.

For the zero-canonical-capacity \(a=14\) cases:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
R&k&k+1&m_{\rm can}\\ \hline
67&55&56&56\\
68&56&57&56\\
69&57&58&57.
\end{array}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Only \(R=67\) permits both components to be saturated canonical lines.
For \(R=68,69\), every reducible-conic circuit has at least one
noncanonical component.

## 6. Irreducible conic normal form

Let \(\nu:\mathbf P^1_\lambda\to C\) normalize the conic. Every ambient
coordinate restricts to a nonzero binary quadratic

\[
z_j(\lambda_0,\lambda_1)
\qquad(1\le j\le a).
\]

Put

\[
\Delta(\lambda)=\prod_{j=1}^a z_j(\lambda)
\]

and, using the source Lagrange basis \(L_j(T)\),

\[
N_C(T,\lambda)
=
\sum_{j=1}^a
L_j(T)\frac{\Delta(\lambda)}{z_j(\lambda)}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
\deg_TN_C\le a-1,
\qquad
\deg_\lambda N_C\le2(a-1).
\tag{6.2}
\]

At every actual circuit point \(\lambda_s\), after monic normalization,

\[
P_s(T)
=
\frac{N_C(T,\lambda_s)}
{[T^{a-1}]N_C(T,\lambda_s)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Hence

\[
t_i\in I_s
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N_C(t_i,\lambda_s)=0.
\tag{6.4}
\]

If some \(N_C(t_i,\lambda)\) vanishes identically, all conic locators
have a common selected root and emit a common-factor precursor.
Otherwise each selected root occurs in at most \(2(a-1)\) conic
locators.

For \(a=12,R=69,m=120\), define

\[
L_C(\lambda)=\prod_{s=1}^{120}(\lambda-\lambda_s),
\qquad
\mathcal R_C(\lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^{69}N_C(t_i,\lambda).
\]

Every circuit point contributes its 11 selected roots, so

\[
\boxed{
L_C^{11}\mid\mathcal R_C,
\qquad
\deg(\mathcal R_C/L_C^{11})\le198.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The number 198 is exact:

\[
69\cdot22-120\cdot11=198.
\]

This is the irreducible-conic analogue of the degree-88 line remainder.

The effective conic source-pole period sharpens this branch. In the
principal row, every common selected root and every root shared by two
coordinate quadratics is impossible. The only surviving endpoint has:

\[
\gcd(z_i,z_j)=1\quad(i\ne j),
\]

an exact period

\[
\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}(12,-24)\simeq\mathcal O_{\mathcal D},
\]

and a degree-nine endpoint section. Its 120 locator supports form an
exact

\[
1\text{-}(60,11,22)
\]

design on 60 selected roots; the other nine selected roots form the
degree-nine remainder locator. For \(a=14,R=68\), the same argument
excludes the complete irreducible-conic branch.

## 7. Cubic complete-intersection branch

The third circuit has exactly \(3k\) selected reciprocal-Cauchy points
in one plane and is cut there by:

\[
\text{a cubic }E
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{a degree-}k\text{ curve }D,
\qquad
\gcd(E,D)=1.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For the hardest row this is a 177-point circuit. Reducible cubics must be
split into line-plus-conic or three-line components and compared with
the exact line and conic thresholds. An irreducible cubic gives a
genus-zero or genus-one selected-record packet.

The complete-intersection equations are already verifier-checkable
geometric data. What remains is a source-specific theorem converting
them into an enabled quotient, planted, field, rank, or saturation owner
at one of the same 177 records.

## 8. Corrected remaining theorem

The high-support obligation should now be stated as:

> **Selected-record low-degree-curve emission.** Every actual
> postcritical circuit supported on a plane conic or on a cubic
> complete intersection as in Theorem 3.1 emits an enabled same-record
> semantic cell.

Together with the complete line exclusion, this reduces every circuit
of size at most \(3k\) to the surviving conic endpoint, reducible-conic
template, or cubic branch.
The residual sparsification statement is only:

> **Postcritical large-circuit reduction.** Every actual postcritical
> relation either contains a circuit of size at most \(3k\), or a
> circuit of size at least \(3k+1\) directly emits an enabled
> same-record semantic cell.

At \(a=12,R=69\), the unclassified circuit range now begins at 178,
instead of 120.

## 9. Boundary

This note does not pay the surviving conic or cubic circuits. It proves
that they are the only intermediate-support geometries omitted by the
line theorem, while the pole-support reduction isolates the exact
principal conic endpoint. Any future high-support proof should consume
these reduced packets rather than treating all supports above \(2k+1\)
as one undifferentiated branch.
