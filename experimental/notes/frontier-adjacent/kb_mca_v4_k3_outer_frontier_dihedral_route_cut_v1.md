## 4. Fixed-point-free deployed dihedral route cut

Let

\[
p=2\,130\,706\,433,\qquad N=2^{21},\qquad
a=1\,213\,133\,211\in\mathbf F_p^\times.
\]

Exact arithmetic gives \(a^N=1\) and \(a^{N/2}=-1\), so \(a\) has order
\(N\).  Put \(D=\langle a\rangle\), the deployed order-\(N\) evaluation
carrier, and define

\[
\tau_1(x)=\frac a x,\qquad \tau_2(x)=\frac {a^3}x.
\]

Both preserve \(D\).  Writing \(x=a^k\), a fixed point of \(\tau_1\) or
\(\tau_2\) would respectively solve

\[
2k\equiv1\pmod N,\qquad 2k\equiv3\pmod N.
\]

Neither congruence is soluble because \(\gcd(2,N)=2\) while both right-hand
sides are odd.  Thus both involutions are fixed-point-free **on \(D\)**.
No claim of fixed-point-freeness on all of \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\) is
made.

The associated degree-two quotients are

\[
q_1(x)=x+\frac a x,\qquad q_2(x)=x+\frac {a^3}x.
\]

More generally, for \(c\in\{a,a^3\}\), put \(q_c(x)=x+c/x\).  In
\(\mathbf F_p(x,T)\),

\[
q_c(T)-q_c(x)
=\frac{(T-x)(Tx-c)}{Tx}.
\]

If \(x\in D\), then both \(x\) and \(c/x\) lie in \(D\), and the
fixed-point-free calculation above says that they are distinct.  Since
\(q_c=(X^2+c)/X\), with \(c\ne0\), its numerator and denominator are
coprime and \(\deg q_c=2\).  The displayed factorization therefore gives
the complete fibre

\[
q_c^{-1}(q_c(x))=\left\{x,\frac c x\right\}
\]

as two distinct reduced points of \(D\).  The value \(q_c(x)\) is finite,
so neither \(0\) nor \(\infty\), the poles of the displayed affine
expression, is an omitted fibre point.  Thus the two carrier pairings are
genuine complete quadratic fibres, not merely pairs on which the
corresponding quotient happens to agree.

Their product is

\[
\tau_2\tau_1(x)=a^2x,
\]

which has order \(N/2=2^{20}\).  Hence

\[
G=\langle\tau_1,\tau_2\rangle
\]

is dihedral of order \(2(N/2)=N=2^{21}\).

Set

\[
u=x^{N/2}-x^{-N/2}.
\]

The identities \(a^{N/2}=(a^3)^{N/2}=-1\) show directly that \(u\) is fixed
by both reflections; \((a^2)^{N/2}=1\) shows rotation invariance.  Moreover

\[
u=\frac{x^N-1}{x^{N/2}}
\]

has rational-map degree \(N\).  Indeed \(N\mid p-1\), so \(X^N-1\) has
all \(N\) roots in \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), and those roots are exactly the
unique subgroup \(D=\langle a\rangle\) of order \(N\).  Since
\(p\nmid N\), its derivative \(NX^{N-1}\) is nonzero at every root.
The denominator is nonzero on \(D\).  Consequently

\[
u^{-1}(0)=D
\]

is a complete reduced degree-\(N\) fibre of cardinality \(N\).  At \(0\)
the numerator is \(-1\), so \(u\) has a pole of order \(N/2\); at
\(\infty\) the numerator degree exceeds the denominator degree by \(N/2\),
so \(u\) has a second pole of order \(N/2\).  Thus the pole divisor also
has total degree \(N\), independently confirming the rational-map degree.
Therefore

\[
\mathbf F_p(u)\subseteq \mathbf F_p(x)^G
\]

and both subfields have index \(N\) in \(\mathbf F_p(x)\): the first by the
map degree, the second by Artin's fixed-field theorem.  Consequently

\[
\mathbf F_p(x)^{\langle\tau_1\rangle}
\cap
\mathbf F_p(x)^{\langle\tau_2\rangle}
=\mathbf F_p(x)^G
=\mathbf F_p(u).
\]

For \(x\in D\), \(x^{N/2}\in\{1,-1\}\), so
\(u=x^{N/2}-x^{-N/2}=0\).  Every nonconstant common invariant is a
nonconstant rational function of \(u\), hence has degree at least \(N\);
its restriction through this generator is constant on \(D\).

This proves a carrier-preservation inference counterexample: two
fixed-point-free, carrier-preserving quadratic folds need not give a
degree-four coarsening or a useful nonconstant carrier quotient.  It does
**not** construct an actual received line, received data, explaining
polynomial, graph record, slope, or source class realizing the two folds.
