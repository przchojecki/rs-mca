### Theorem 5.1: coordinate plus Krylov-chain form

There are:

- a coordinate set \(S\subseteq[69]\);
- positive integers \(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_h\);
- vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_h\in F^{69}\);

such that

\[
\boxed{
C
=
F^S
\oplus
\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^h
\operatorname{span}
\{v_\alpha,\mathsf Tv_\alpha,\ldots,
  \mathsf T^{\epsilon_\alpha-1}v_\alpha\},
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
r_C=|S|+\sum_{\alpha=1}^h\epsilon_\alpha.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Moreover

\[
C+C\mathsf T
=
F^S
\oplus
\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^h
\operatorname{span}
\{v_\alpha,\mathsf Tv_\alpha,\ldots,
  \mathsf T^{\epsilon_\alpha}v_\alpha\}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

The displayed sums are vector-space direct sums. The supports of different
Krylov generators need not be disjoint. Thus (5.5) is an exact Kronecker
classification, not an unjustified partition into disjoint GRS blocks.

#### Proof

Consider the pencil of maps

\[
\mathcal P(z):
C\longrightarrow C+C\mathsf T,
\qquad
c\longmapsto c\mathsf T-zc.
\tag{5.8}
\]

It has full column rank over \(F(z)\). Indeed, if
\((\mathsf T-zI)c(z)=0\), then coordinatewise

\[
(t_i-z)c_i(z)=0,
\]

and hence \(c_i(z)=0\) for every \(i\).

The Kronecker canonical form therefore has no right singular blocks. A
regular summand gives a subspace \(C_0\subseteq C\) on which both inclusion
and multiplication by \(\mathsf T\) take values in the same
\(\dim C_0\)-dimensional summand. Inclusion is an isomorphism there, so
\(\mathsf T C_0\subseteq C_0\). Because \(\mathsf T\) is diagonal with
distinct eigenvalues, every invariant subspace is a coordinate subspace.
The regular part is therefore \(F^S\), split into one-dimensional
eigenvalue blocks.

The remaining blocks are the left singular blocks
\(L_{\epsilon_\alpha}^{\mathsf T}\). There are exactly

\[
\dim(C+C\mathsf T)-\dim C=h
\]

of them, and no zero-length block occurs because
\(C+C\mathsf T\) is generated by the two pencil maps. In one such block
there are domain vectors \(c_0,\ldots,c_{\epsilon-1}\) and codomain vectors
\(y_0,\ldots,y_\epsilon\) with

\[
c_j=y_j,
\qquad
c_j\mathsf T=y_{j+1}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

For \(j<\epsilon-1\), the second identity and the next first identity give

\[
c_{j+1}=c_j\mathsf T.
\]

Taking \(v=c_0\) produces the corresponding chain in (5.5)-(5.7).
Summing the canonical blocks proves the theorem. \(\square\)
