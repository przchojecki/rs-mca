base-rational vector \(v\).

Let \(P_{v,h}\) be the degree-at-most-\(e\) representative of \(vu_h\).
Because \(Xv\in U_\Sigma\), the degree-at-most-\(e\) representative
\(P_{Xv,h}\) also exists. The polynomial

\[
XP_{v,h}-P_{Xv,h}
\]

vanishes on \(\Sigma\) and has degree at most \(e+1<s\). It is zero, so

\[
\deg P_{v,h}\le e-1,\qquad P_{Xv,h}=XP_{v,h}.
\tag{4.45c}
\]

The vectors \(v,Xv\) are independent. Indeed, if \(Xv=cv\), then \(v\)
vanishes at every source point other than \(c\). Both polynomials in
(4.45c) would have at least \(s-1>e-1\) zeros and hence would be zero.
Pointwise nonvanishing of the translated source pair would force \(v=0\),
a contradiction. Thus

\[
C_v=\langle v,Xv\rangle_B
\quad\text{has dimension two,}\qquad
\dim_B(U_\Sigma/C_v)=1.
\tag{4.45d}
\]

Choose the first base vector \(q_\circ\in U_\Sigma(B)\setminus C_v\).
Every actual base projective locator direction has one of the forms

\[
q=(b+cX)v
\quad\text{or}\quad
q=q_\circ+(b+cX)v,
\qquad b,c\in B.
\tag{4.45e}
\]

Put

\[
P=\operatorname{rep}_{\le e-1}(v(u_0,u_1)),
\qquad
P_\circ=\operatorname{rep}_{\le e}(q_\circ(u_0,u_1)).
\]

No-wrap and (4.45c) give the polynomial identities

\[
P_q=(b+cX)P
\quad\text{or}\quad
P_q=P_\circ+(b+cX)P.
\tag{4.45f}
\]

Let \(x\in D\setminus\Sigma\) be the actual moving root of \(q\). In the
first case, moving-root transversality makes \(b+cx\ne0\), and the selected
slope lies in the source-map image of \(P\), indexed by \(t=\infty\). In
the second case it lies in the source-map image of

\[
P_\circ+tP,\qquad t=b+cx\in B.
\tag{4.45g}
\]

This uses the same actual locator and the same moving root; no owner or
syndrome substitution occurs. There are exactly \(p+1\) possible maps.
Each has image size at most \(n-s\), so

\[
\boxed{
\#\{\text{selected slopes in the occupied span-three branch}\}
\le(p+1)(n-s)
=4{,}180{,}884{,}949{,}033{,}404
<B_{\rm rem}.}
\tag{4.45h}
\]

Consequently both alternatives in (4.45), including the sharp three-petal
family, are directly paid. The spread/petal trichotomy remains a useful
regression for the actual-locator interface but is no longer an open
payment.

### 4.5 Four-locator collective-rank closure

There is a further exact reduction when the occupied actual locators span all
four dimensions of \(W_\Sigma\). Choose four independent actual units

\[
q_0,q_1,q_2,q_3.
\tag{4.46}
\]

For each \(i\), the other three locators are independent. A
reciprocal-dimension-two triple would put the fixed translated source plane
in the active C5/base cell and delete the whole line. On a post-C5 survivor,
all four coordinate triples therefore have reciprocal dimension at least
three. Applying the quadratic-adjugate theorem to each triple gives

\[
\sum_{j\ne i}\ell_{ij}(X)q_j=0
\quad\text{in }A_\Sigma,
\qquad \deg\ell_{ij}\le1.
\tag{4.47}
\]

Every \(\ell_{ij}\) is nonzero. Otherwise the Venn-factor argument for that
triple gives a one-root exchange between two actual locators, contradicting
(4.37). Put the four relations into the canonical zero-diagonal matrix

\[
\mathcal L(X)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\ell_{01}&\ell_{02}&\ell_{03}\\
\ell_{10}&0&\ell_{12}&\ell_{13}\\
\ell_{20}&\ell_{21}&0&\ell_{23}\\
\ell_{30}&\ell_{31}&\ell_{32}&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.48}
\]

Fixed row reduction and primitive row normalization make \(\mathcal L\)
verifier-checkable from the four actual locators. At every source point,

\[
\mathcal L(h)
\begin{pmatrix}
q_0(h)\\q_1(h)\\q_2(h)\\q_3(h)
\end{pmatrix}
=0.
\tag{4.49}
\]

All four locators are source units, so the vector in (4.49) is nonzero.
Therefore \(\det\mathcal L\) vanishes at all \(s\) source points. Its degree
is at most four, and \(s>4\), hence

\[
\det\mathcal L=0.
\tag{4.50}
\]

The generic rank cannot be one. A rank-one matrix over \(F(X)\) factors as
\(ab^T\). Nonvanishing of every off-diagonal entry forces every component of
\(a\) and \(b\) to be nonzero, while the zero diagonal forces
\(a_i b_i=0\) for every \(i\), a contradiction.

The generic rank cannot be three either. Suppose it were three. A primitive
right-kernel vector

\[
\alpha(X)=(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)^T
\tag{4.51}
\]

can be obtained from one nonzero column of
\(\operatorname{adj}\mathcal L\), after removing its polynomial gcd. Thus

\[
\deg\alpha_i\le3.
\tag{4.52}
\]

Choose a nonzero \(3\times3\) minor \(D(X)\). Off the source set

\[
E=Z_\Sigma(D),\qquad |E|\le3,
\tag{4.53}
\]

the kernel of \(\mathcal L(h)\) is one-dimensional. Hence there is a scalar
\(c(h)\) such that

\[
q_i(h)=c(h)\alpha_i(h)
\quad(i=0,1,2,3;\ h\in\Sigma\setminus E).
\tag{4.54}
\]

The four polynomials \(\alpha_i\) are linearly independent. Indeed, a
constant dependence among them would give a nonzero
\(q=\sum_i d_iq_i\in W_\Sigma\) vanishing on at least

\[
s-3=2e-5>e
\]

source points. Both degree-at-most-\(e\) representatives of \(qu_0\) and
\(qu_1\) would then be zero. Since the translated source pair has no common
source zero, \(q=0\) in \(A_\Sigma\), contradicting independence of the
\(q_i\). Therefore the \(\alpha_i\) form a basis of \(F[X]_{\le3}\).

Use this basis to write \(q_P\in W_\Sigma\) for the constant linear
combination corresponding to \(P\in F[X]_{\le3}\). Equation (4.54) becomes

\[
q_P(h)=c(h)P(h)
\quad(h\in\Sigma\setminus E).
\tag{4.55}
\]

This is the punctured cubic cyclic space forced by a generic-rank-three
matrix.

Because \(1\in W_\Sigma\), there is \(T\in F[X]_{\le3}\) with
\(1=q_T\). On the good source points, \(1=cT\). Let \(q_{\bf1}\) denote
the element corresponding to the polynomial \(1\), and put

\[
A_r=\operatorname{rep}_{\le e}(q_{\bf1}u_r),
\qquad r=0,1.
\]

Then \(TA_r-u_r\) has degree at most \(e+3\) and vanishes on at least
\(2e-5\) points. Since \(e>8\), this is more roots than its degree, so
\(TA_r=u_r\) as a polynomial identity. Coprimality of \(u_0,u_1\) forces
\(T\) to be constant.

Apply the same argument to \(q_{X^3}\). If
\(B_r=\operatorname{rep}_{\le e}(q_{X^3}u_r)\), then

\[
TB_r=X^3u_r.
\tag{4.56}
\]

The nonzero constant \(T\) and \(\deg B_r\le e\) force
\(\deg u_r\le e-3\) for both source coordinates, contradicting the exact
projective degree \(e\). Thus generic rank three is impossible.

Combining (4.50) with the rank-one and rank-three exclusions gives

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{F(X)}\mathcal L=2.}
\tag{4.57}
\]

This is a baseline-free collective rank loss: each row is rooted at three
actual selected locators, the comparison rank is taken after imposing all
ordinary witness equations, and the rank drop is not a direct sum of
per-witness interpolation defects. It yields the exact occupancy split

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
\dim\langle\mathscr Q\rangle\le2
&\Longrightarrow\text{direct payment};\\
\dim\langle\mathscr Q\rangle=3
&\Longrightarrow\text{spread-or-petal packet (4.45)};\\
\dim\langle\mathscr Q\rangle=4
&\Longrightarrow\text{rank-two collective linear-syzygy packet (4.57).}
\end{array}}
\tag{4.58}
\]

Equation (4.57) is structural emission, not yet a slope payment. A successor
collective-rank owner must still bound the complete selected-slope projection
of this packet or synchronize it with an already-paid pair-global owner.

### 4.6 The rank-two rows come from the lowered source pencil

The collective matrix has a more intrinsic interpretation. Define the
degree-lowered multiplier space

\[
V_\Sigma=
\{q\in W_\Sigma:
q u_0,q u_1\text{ have representatives of degree at most }e-1\}.
\tag{4.59}
\]

This space is fixed by the translated source pair. It contains no actual
locator choice.

Choose any basis \(q_0,\ldots,q_3\) of \(W_\Sigma\). A degree-one source
syzygy

\[
\sum_{i=0}^3(a_i+Xb_i)q_i=0
\tag{4.60}
\]

is equivalent to one element of \(V_\Sigma\). Indeed, put

\[
q_a=\sum_i a_iq_i,\qquad q_b=\sum_i b_iq_i.
\]

Equation (4.60) says \(q_a=-Xq_b\), so both \(q_b\) and \(Xq_b\) belong to
\(W_\Sigma\). If \(A_r\) and \(C_r\) are the degree-at-most-\(e\)
representatives of \(q_bu_r\) and \(Xq_bu_r\), then

\[
XA_r-C_r
\]

vanishes on \(\Sigma\) and has degree at most \(e+1<s=2e-2\). It is
therefore zero, and \(\deg A_r\le e-1\). Thus \(q_b\in V_\Sigma\).
Conversely, \(q\in V_\Sigma\) implies \(Xq\in W_\Sigma\), so the coordinate
vectors of \(q\) and \(Xq\) give a relation of the form (4.60). Hence

\[
\boxed{
\{\text{degree-one syzygies on }W_\Sigma\}
\simeq V_\Sigma.}
\tag{4.61}
\]

The four rows of \(\mathcal L\) and (4.57) imply
\(\dim_FV_\Sigma\ge2\).

If \(\dim_FV_\Sigma\ge3\), the leading-coefficient map from the associated
degree-at-most-\((e-1)\) polynomial pairs to \(F^2\) has a nonzero kernel.
Consequently there is a nonzero \(v\in V_\Sigma\) such that

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rep}(vu_0),\operatorname{rep}(vu_1)
\text{ both have degree at most }e-2.}
\tag{4.62}
\]

This is an exact low-degree source-multiplier emission. It is not by itself
an active source-rational payment: \(v\) may vanish at source points, and
even a source-compatible auxiliary rational map must still be synchronized
with the selected slopes of the actual upper-stratum locator.

Suppose instead that

\[
\dim_FV_\Sigma=2.
\tag{4.63}
\]

Let \(v_0,v_1\) be a basis. Write

\[
Xv_r=\sum_i c_{ri}q_i,\qquad
v_r=\sum_i d_{ri}q_i.
\]

Then the two canonical rows

\[
\mathcal R_r(X)=(c_{r0}-Xd_{r0},\ldots,c_{r3}-Xd_{r3})
\qquad(r=0,1)
\tag{4.64}
\]

form the entire degree-one syzygy space. Consequently the four-locator
matrix has a constant-left factorization

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L(X)=U\,\mathcal R(X),\qquad
U\in F^{4\times2},\quad
\operatorname{rank}U=2,}
\tag{4.65}
\]

where \(\mathcal R\) has the two rows in (4.64). In particular the
collective rank loss is not an arbitrary rank-two pencil: it factors through
the first shift prolongation of a two-dimensional degree-lowered source
pencil.

The two polynomial pairs

\[
(A_r,B_r)=\operatorname{rep}_{\le e-1}(v_r(u_0,u_1))
\qquad(r=0,1)
\tag{4.66}
\]

have cross determinant of degree at most \(2e-2=s\) which vanishes on
\(\Sigma\). Therefore exactly one of the following holds:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
A_0B_1-A_1B_0=0,
&\text{a polynomial rank-one lowered-pencil packet};\\[1mm]
A_0B_1-A_1B_0=c\Lambda_\Sigma,\ c\in F^\times,
&\text{a saturated first-prolongation packet.}
\end{array}}
\tag{4.67}
\]

If every element of \(V_\Sigma\) vanishes at one source point, all four
polynomials in (4.66) have the corresponding linear factor. The determinant
has a double zero there, so the squarefree second alternative in (4.67) is
impossible. Thus the common-source-zero packet lies inside the polynomial
rank-one branch.

The rank-four collective packet has therefore sharpened to the exact split

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
\dim V_\Sigma\ge3
&\Longrightarrow\text{degree-}(e-2)
\text{ source-multiplier packet (4.62)};\\[1mm]
\dim V_\Sigma=2,\ \det=0
&\Longrightarrow\text{rank-one/common-source-zero lowered pencil};\\[1mm]
\dim V_\Sigma=2,\ \det\ne0
&\Longrightarrow\text{saturated first prolongation (4.65)--(4.67).}
\end{array}}
\tag{4.68}
\]

The finite \(\mathbf F_{13}\) control is consistent with the first branch
in every one of its six all-coordinate-rank-excess rows. In all six,
the full degree-one syzygy space has dimension two, its slope-coordinate
map has rank two, it has no common source zero, and its lowered-pair
determinant is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(\Lambda_\Sigma\). The bounded
polynomial
kernel dimensions of \(\mathcal L\) in degrees \(0,1,2\) are

```text
right: 0,2,4
left:  2,4,6
```

so the observed Kronecker type has two constant left null vectors and two
right minimal indices equal to one. This is finite evidence for the
first-prolongation branch, not a proof that the other alternatives in
(4.68) are empty.

### 4.7 Transverse first prolongations are directly paid

Multiplication by \(X\) is injective on \(V_\Sigma\). If \(Xv=0\) in the
source algebra, each degree-at-most-\((e-1)\) representative of \(vu_r\)
vanishes on every nonzero source point. There are at least \(s-1>e-1\)
such points, so both representatives are zero. At a possible source point
\(0\), the translated source pair is not simultaneously zero, forcing
\(v(0)=0\) as well. Hence \(v=0\).

Assume

\[
\dim V_\Sigma=2,\qquad
V_\Sigma\cap XV_\Sigma=0.
\tag{4.69}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{W_\Sigma=V_\Sigma\oplus XV_\Sigma.}
\tag{4.70}
\]

The four actual base locators make \(W_\Sigma\) base-defined, and two
independent actual-triple relations make \(V_\Sigma\) base-defined. Thus
every actual locator \(q\in W_\Sigma(B)\) has a unique decomposition

\[
q=a+Xb,\qquad a,b\in V_\Sigma(B).
\tag{4.71}
\]

For \(v\in V_\Sigma(B)\), let

\[
P_v=(P_{v,0},P_{v,1})
=\operatorname{rep}_{\le e-1}(v(u_0,u_1)).
\tag{4.72}
\]

The degree bounds make the source identity in (4.71) an ordinary
polynomial identity:

\[
P_q=P_a+XP_b.
\tag{4.73}
\]

Let \(x\in V=D\setminus\Sigma\) be the actual moving root of a selected
slope on the locator \(q\). Evaluation of (4.73) gives

\[
P_q(x)=P_{a+xb}(x).
\tag{4.74}
\]

The vector on the right is nonzero, since the selected moving-root equation
requires \(P_q(x)\ne(0,0)\). Because \(x\in D\subset B\),
\(a+xb\) is a base point of \(V_\Sigma\). The selected slope is therefore
in the finite source-map image of one of the \(p+1\) projective points of
\(\mathbf P(V_\Sigma(B))\). Hence the complete transverse span-four branch
has cap

\[
\boxed{
(p+1)(n-s)
=4{,}180{,}884{,}949{,}033{,}404
<B_{\rm rem}.}
\tag{4.75}
\]

This is same-locator containment: (4.74) starts from the actual locator and
its actual moving root. No auxiliary source map is charged without proving
that it contains that slope.

Every span-four packet not paid by (4.75) emits a three-step multiplier.
Indeed, \(\dim V_\Sigma\ge2\). If its dimension is two and (4.69) fails,
or if its dimension is at least three, the dimension formula and injectivity
of \(X\) give a nonzero

\[
w\in V_\Sigma\cap XV_\Sigma.
\]

Write \(w=Xv\) with \(v\in V_\Sigma\). Since \(w\in V_\Sigma\),

\[
\boxed{
v,\ Xv,\ X^2v\in W_\Sigma.}
\tag{4.76}
\]

Applying the no-wrap argument twice gives

\[
\boxed{
\deg\operatorname{rep}(vu_0),
\deg\operatorname{rep}(vu_1)\le e-2.}
\tag{4.77}
\]

Consequently the dimension-four branch is now

\[
\boxed{
\text{direct transverse first-prolongation payment}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\text{degree-}(e-2)\text{ three-step multiplier packet}.}
\tag{4.78}
\]

At this point (4.77) is only structural; it does not by itself synchronize
an auxiliary low-degree map with an actual selected slope. The next step
uses the whole cyclic three-space and the actual moving root to obtain that
synchronization.

### 4.8 The three-step packet also has only \(p+1\) images

The vectors in (4.76) are linearly independent. Otherwise a nonzero
polynomial \(T\) of degree at most two would satisfy

\[
T(X)v=0
\quad\text{in }A_\Sigma.
\]

The multiplier \(v\) would vanish at all but at most two source points.
Each polynomial in (4.77) would then vanish at at least \(s-2>e-2\)
points, so both would be zero. Since the source pair is not simultaneously
zero at any source point, this would force \(v=0\), a contradiction.
Therefore

\[
\boxed{
C_v=\langle v,Xv,X^2v\rangle_B
\text{ has dimension three}.}
\tag{4.79}
\]

The spaces \(W_\Sigma\), \(V_\Sigma\), their intersection, and the
preimage defining the three-step vectors are all kernels of base-linear
maps once four actual base locators span \(W_\Sigma\). Choose the first
nonzero three-step vector in fixed row-reduced base order. Then \(C_v\) is
pair-global and base-defined. Choose the first

\[
q_\circ\in W_\Sigma(B)\setminus C_v.
\tag{4.80}
\]

Every base projective locator direction has one of the forms

\[
q=p(X)v
\quad\text{or}\quad
q=q_\circ+p(X)v,
\qquad p\in B[X]_{\le2}.
\tag{4.81}
\]

Put

\[
P=\operatorname{rep}_{\le e-2}(v(u_0,u_1)),
\qquad
P_\circ=\operatorname{rep}_{\le e}(q_\circ(u_0,u_1)).
\tag{4.82}
\]

The degree bounds and \(e<s\) turn (4.81) into ordinary polynomial
identities

\[
P_q=pP
\quad\text{or}\quad
P_q=P_\circ+pP.
\tag{4.83}
\]

For \(t\in B\), define the finite carrier image of the pair
\(P_\circ+tP\), and define the \(t=\infty\) image from \(P\). Common zeros
and poles are omitted exactly as in the first-gap image owner. There are
only \(p+1\) such maps.

Let \(x\in D\setminus\Sigma\) be an actual moving root. If the actual
locator lies in \(C_v\), (4.83) puts its selected slope in the
\(t=\infty\) image. Otherwise scale its direction as in the second form of
(4.81); evaluation of (4.83) puts the slope in the image indexed by

\[
t=p(x)\in B.
\tag{4.84}
\]

The evaluated pair is nonzero by moving-root transversality. This proves
same-locator containment in a fixed union of \(p+1\) images. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\#\{\text{selected slopes in the complete span-four branch}\}
\le(p+1)(n-s)
=4{,}180{,}884{,}949{,}033{,}404
<B_{\rm rem}.}
\tag{4.85}
\]

The transverse packet (4.75) and the three-step packet (4.85) have the
same cap and are alternatives of one pair-global source space. They are
not added. Consequently:

\[
\boxed{\text{every occupied actual-locator span-four branch is paid}.}
\tag{4.86}
\]
